## Top Takeaways
- Anthropic's draft IPO filing shows first-half revenue up roughly fifteenfold to about $16B, while compute spending nearly quadrupled. Most of the headline loss is a non-cash accounting charge, not operating losses.
- Anthropic has committed more than $600B to cloud and chip suppliers, mostly on terms it can't cancel. That's multi-year visible revenue for Google, Amazon, Microsoft and Broadcom, though two customers drive about a fifth of Anthropic's own sales.
- Nvidia has more than $170B of guarantees and investments tied to smaller AI cloud providers, and is in talks to support Firmus after that company shelved its IPO. Nvidia is effectively financing part of its own demand.
- Australia's NextDC lost up to 44% in a day after a power delay at a key site. The constraint on AI data centers is grid connections and permits, not a lack of tenants.
- Nscale reports about $103B of contracts, yet only about 2.5% is live. Neocloud backlogs depend on financing and on-time delivery.
- SpaceX's cloud business brought in $7.6B last quarter, and it plans to roughly double capex next quarter. Tesla and SpaceX have set up board committees to study a merger, with no terms agreed.
- More Gulf oil is flowing than before the war, but shipping and insurance costs keep crude near $110. France's 10-year yield reached 5% and UK inflation rose to 3.5%, which keeps pressure on long-term rates.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Anthropic H1 revenue: about $16.3B, versus $1.1B a year earlier.
- Nvidia's exposure to AI cloud providers: more than $170B.
- Nscale: $103B contracted, about $2.6B active.
- SpaceX Q4 capex guide: $35–40B, versus $19.6B in Q3.
- French 10-year yield: 5%, the first time since 2002.
- H100 rental index: up 4.7% on the day to about $3.12 an hour.
