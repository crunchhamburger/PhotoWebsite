## Top Takeaways
- Anthropic sold shares at $83 each, above its planned range, and raised $81 billion, the largest IPO ever. The stock ended its first day about 17% higher, well below where it opened.
- The wider market did not join in. The S&P 500 fell about 2%, its sixth straight loss, and every sector was down. One explanation offered is that small investors sold other AI stocks to buy the new one.
- How big Anthropic is depends on the share count. Including shares that could still be issued, its value at the open was above $2 trillion, against roughly $1.5 trillion on shares already issued.
- A group of large investment firms promised in August to raise $500 billion to help Nvidia's customers buy chips. A report says none of that money has been put to work yet.
- AMD fell almost 8% after a short-seller questioned its ties to a blacklisted Chinese server maker. AMD rejects the claims.
- US export policy stayed in focus. One report says Nvidia chip licences for China were hurried through, and another says TSMC and Samsung paused some shipments to Chinese customers under a new rule.
- Federal Reserve minutes show nearly all officials expect more rate rises.
- JPMorgan is reported to be offering software buyout bonds at a steep discount.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Anthropic: 980 million shares at $83; first-day close of $97.17.
- Total raised with a private share sale: at least $107 billion.
- S&P 500: down 2.0% to 7,175.83. Dow: down about 1,100 points.
- AMD: down 7.7%. Nvidia: down 3.3%.
- Nvidia's guarantee limit: 25% of a project's cost.
- December quarter-point Fed hike: priced at about 95%.
- Software buyout bonds: near 80 cents on the dollar, a 12% to 13% yield.
- Hormuz bypass cost, per the IEA: about $240 billion.
