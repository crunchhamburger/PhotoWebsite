## Top Takeaways
- Nvidia's independent directors are reviewing reports that its chief executive was targeted by Chinese intelligence through a personal relationship with an executive at a Singapore chip customer. The board says it has seen no sign of leaked secrets.
- Nvidia shares fell roughly 6–7.5% early Monday; outlets reported different figures. The worry is key-person risk at a company run unusually flat, and a weaker hand in Washington on China chip exports.
- Last Thursday's tech slide was deepened by rule-based funds cutting stock exposure at the fastest pace since early 2020.
- AI companies have sold more than $300B of new stock this year, with about $100B more lined up. Lower share prices make each dollar raised more dilutive.
- OpenAI cut its funding round in both size and valuation. That lowers the bar for Anthropic's IPO, planned for the week of Nov 16.
- SoftBank's borrowing headroom is shrinking as its AI holdings fall in value. It reports results Tuesday.
- TSMC is reported to be studying a move into stacked memory chips. It would be a long-term threat to today's memory makers, not a 2027 one.
- Monday's rebound left Korean memory stocks behind, and oil fell on lower odds of new US strikes on Iran.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- AI stock sold in 2026: over $300B, against $194B for all US IPOs and follow-ons in 2025.
- OpenAI: $11B raised at $1.25T pre-money; round target cut to $20B from $30B.
- Last week: S&P 500 about −2.5%, tech sector about −7%.
- SoftBank estimated loan-to-value: 18–22%, against a 25% ceiling.
- Brent crude: down about 5% to near $94.
- China October producer prices: +5.7% a year, a four-year high.
