## Top Takeaways
- Alphabet, Amazon and Meta burned about $45 billion of free cash in the third quarter as Big Tech's quarterly capex reached about $207 billion. The four largest spenders lost close to $900 billion of market value after reporting.
- Lenders are now setting the terms for AI: neocloud borrowers are effectively shut out of bond markets, and OpenAI cut its funding round to about $20 billion.
- Anthropic may receive a junk credit rating after its IPO, which would keep its computing plans dependent on guarantees from stronger companies.
- Memory makers are the winners: SK Hynix says it is sold out to 2029, and South Korea's October exports nearly doubled from a year earlier. The losers are PC makers: Intel fell 29% in a day.
- The Fed held rates but two officials voted to raise them; markets see a December hike as likely.
- US crude rose about 15% in October on fears of renewed conflict with Iran after the midterms.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Nasdaq −2.0%, S&P 500 −0.6%, Dow −0.9%, Russell 2000 +1.3% on the week.
- 10-year Treasury about 4.94%, down roughly 35 basis points in October.
- Big Tech third-quarter capex: about $207 billion, up from $113 billion a year earlier.
- Global PC shipments: down 21% in the third quarter.
- US third-quarter GDP: +3.4% annualized.
- WTI crude: about $104 a barrel.
