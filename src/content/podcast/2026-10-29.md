## Top Takeaways
- Alphabet's stock dropped sharply after it reported a quarter of negative free cash flow, about –$9B, as data-center spending climbed and search growth cooled to 12%. Its cloud unit doubled in size.
- Microsoft spent less than analysts expected, but blamed late data-center deliveries rather than a pullback. Azure grew 48%, and the shares rose.
- Samsung's chip profit rose about fifteen-fold and it raised this year's investment by roughly 60%. The stock still fell because it postponed details of shareholder payouts.
- Flash memory now costs about five times what it did a year ago, as cloud giants outbuy Apple, and Apple's iPhone prices have risen.
- The H100 rental index gave back Tuesday's jump, falling about 17% to under $3 an hour.
- The Fed held rates steady, but two officials wanted a hike, and markets now see a December increase as likely. US growth came in at 3.4% for the third quarter.
- Oil stays above $100 on fears of a renewed Iran conflict after the midterms. Shell and TotalEnergies raised buybacks.
- DoorDash agreed to buy Instacart for about $17bn, subject to antitrust review.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Alphabet quarterly free cash flow: about –$9B; full-year capex plan up to $205B.
- Alphabet cloud revenue: about $31B, roughly double a year ago.
- Microsoft Azure revenue: about $33B, up 48%.
- Samsung chip operating profit: about $80bn.
- Flash memory prices: about 5x in a year.
- H100 rental index: about $2.96 an hour, down 17% in a day.
- Fed target range: 3.75–4.00%; December hike odds about 85%.
- US third-quarter GDP growth: 3.4% annualized.
