## Top Takeaways
- Anthropic's shares fell back on Friday after a 17% rise on their first day. Reports put the drop between roughly 10% and 13%.
- Selling spread beyond chipmakers. Memory and storage companies such as SanDisk, Western Digital and Seagate were among the biggest fallers in the S&P 500.
- Anthropic has heavy commitments for computing capacity, and it reportedly told IPO investors it still needs more, at higher short-term prices. It does not expect positive cash flow before 2028.
- A new US rule shuts the route Chinese AI labs used to rent Nvidia chips in Southeast Asian data centres. One lab, Moonshot, cancelled a $150 million deal in Indonesia.
- California opened its own investigation into Nvidia, adding to a federal probe.
- Lenders are getting more cautious with AI projects. Alphabet paused some municipal energy deals, and lenders to SpaceX want limits on where their chip collateral can go.
- Oil looks settled in a higher range, and inflation is no longer just about energy, which keeps a December Fed hike likely.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Anthropic: up 17% on debut day; worth about $1.76 trillion fully diluted at Thursday's close.
- S&P 500: down 2.0% on Thursday, its sixth straight fall.
- Offshore chip rule: a US licence is needed above about 300 top-end chips.
- Moonshot's cancelled deal: $150 million.
- Alphabet's paused muni deals: $3 billion.
- Brent crude: a forming consensus of $85 to $95 a barrel.
- December quarter-point Fed hike: priced at about 96%.
