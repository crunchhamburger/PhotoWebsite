## Top Takeaways
- Meta now says its own cash flow won't cover its AI plans. It intends to raise outside money early, before rivals soak up investor appetite, and its future commitments rose to about $825B from about $700B in July.
- Markets read that as a warning for the whole AI build-out. Meta lost about 7%, chip stocks about 7% and the Nasdaq 2.6%, while money moved into Treasurys and the 10-year yield fell to just under 5%.
- Meta is adding a nonvoting share class, which works like a 2-for-1 split. It gives the company a way to sell stock for funding without diluting the founder's control.
- Private AI valuations are slipping. OpenAI's new round is set at up to $1.25T, below the $1.4T it pitched earlier this month, after softer investor demand.
- The AI-infrastructure IPO window is narrowing. SB Energy pushed back its listing and SK Hynix dropped a US IPO for Solidigm. Anthropic's upcoming debut is the test everyone is watching.
- Tesla cut its 2026 capex plan to about $20B and its 2030 target to 5M cars, which helps near-term cash but shifts the story toward AI and a possible SpaceX merger.
- Blackstone's exits fell by about a third, but AI-related lending lifted its credit arm about 43%. Private credit is where AI financing is now flowing.
- Brent rose about 4% to around $113 after attacks on Saudi Arabia, which keeps inflation risk alive even as AI worries pull yields lower.
