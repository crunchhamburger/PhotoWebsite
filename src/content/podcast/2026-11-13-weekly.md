## Top Takeaways
- Nvidia's founder and chief executive resigned, and its finance chief took over as interim. The stock lost about $573 billion of value in the week, the largest weekly loss recorded for any company.
- The bigger issue is financing. Nvidia's investments and guarantees supported borrowing across the AI build-out, and the new boss is the executive who resisted the largest of them. Companies that leaned on that support fell hardest.
- Washington is moving to close the Southeast Asian route by which advanced chips reached Chinese customers, through new licence rules and bills tied to the defence budget.
- October inflation came in above forecast. The 10-year Treasury yield rose about 29 basis points to 5.15%, and markets now price three Fed hikes by June.
- Demand held up: CoreWeave's backlog grew and it found cheaper secured funding, and GPU rental prices ended the week slightly higher.
- Anthropic's IPO, set at $70–78 a share, prices on Nov 18 and is expected to come in above the range.
- Stocks still rose because money rotated from technology into staples, healthcare and utilities rather than leaving the market.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- S&P 500 +2.5%, Nasdaq 100 +3.5%, Dow +2.1%, Russell 2000 +1.3% on the week; Nvidia −13%.
- 10-year Treasury 5.15%; 2-year about 4.88%.
- October CPI +0.4% month on month, core +0.3%; producer prices +6.5% year on year.
- Anthropic: 825 million shares at $70–78, plus a $26 billion private placement.
- Data-center supplier stocks: about $4.2 trillion of value lost since June.
- Brent crude about $96 a barrel, WTI about $89; the oil market showed a surplus of almost 2 million barrels a day in October.
