## Top Takeaways
- Meta warned that capital for AI is getting scarce as rivals raise at the same time, and it is creating nonvoting shares so it can sell stock. Big Tech is moving from paying for data centers with its own cash to relying on markets.
- Thursday's AI selloff took about 7% off Meta and the chip index, while the 10-year Treasury yield had its biggest one-day drop since March 2023.
- Brent crude reached about $117, its highest since April, on fears of a wider Iran war, and pushed inflation expectations back up.
- Two chipmakers, Nvidia and Micron, account for about a third of expected S&P 500 profit growth this quarter.
- Elon Musk opened a review of merging Tesla into SpaceX, a combination worth about $3.5 trillion.
- The listing window for AI hardware stayed shut: SK Hynix dropped a US IPO and SB Energy delayed its own.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- S&P 500 −0.5%, Nasdaq −0.7%, Dow +0.4%, Russell 2000 +2% on the week.
- 10-year Treasury: about 4.99% on Friday, down about 10 basis points on the week.
- Meta's future spending commitments: about $825 billion.
- Anthropic first-half revenue: about $16.3 billion, up from $1.1 billion.
- France 10-year yield: above 5% for the first time since 2002.
- Initial jobless claims: 194,000, a three-month low.
