## Top Takeaways
- Weekly credit-default-swap trading on big AI borrowers rose from under $2bn in July to over $9bn in September. With about $466bn of AI debt issued this year, investors are hedging through credit, and Oracle's protection cost has more than doubled since January.
- CoreWeave's five-year CDS sits around 1,090bp, which signals real refinancing risk for a GPU fleet built on debt while rates stay high.
- Korean data center builder Elice fell 32% on its first trading day despite pricing at the top of its range. After Firmus pulled its IPO, that's a poor sign for Nscale's planned listing.
- Korea's Kospi dropped 3.7% as SK Hynix and Samsung sold off, even though memory prices themselves are steady. These stocks are trading on AI sentiment, not on pricing.
- DeepSeek's newest model rivals a top OpenAI model on an agent benchmark at a small fraction of the price, which pressures API pricing and frontier-lab returns (our inference).
- Brent above $110 and a 10-year yield near 5.14% keep the Fed tight and squeeze the IPO and private-credit money AI builders depend on.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- AI-linked debt issuance: about $466bn this year, versus about $101bn a year earlier.
- Oracle five-year CDS: about 322bp, up from 144bp in January.
- Elice: −32% on debut.
- H100 rental index: +6% on the day to about $2.98 an hour, still −6% on the week.
- Yttrium outside China: up about 179x in two years, a risk for chip tooling and aerospace.
