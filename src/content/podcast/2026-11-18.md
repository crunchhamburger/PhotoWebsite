## Top Takeaways
- Nvidia's quarterly revenue more than doubled to about $116 billion and beat forecasts, and it guided to roughly $130 billion for the current quarter. Demand for AI hardware held up through the change of leadership.
- The cost side got worse. Nvidia expects lower profit margins next quarter because memory is scarce and expensive, and it has now promised suppliers about $390 billion, mostly to secure memory.
- The board said it will not bring Jensen Huang back as chief executive. US securities regulators are also looking at the company's disclosures.
- Reports say Nvidia sales staff once introduced Alibaba to a Singapore customer now under criminal investigation, and that large banks lent to a data-center operator that housed that customer. Renting chips offshore was legal at the time.
- Anthropic's IPO is expected to price tonight above its original $70 to $78 range and start trading Thursday. Published valuation estimates differ, from about $1.4 trillion upward.
- Microsoft is reported to be near a deal worth about $500 million to supply OpenAI models to ByteDance through its cloud.
- The European Central Bank warned that the debt behind the AI build-out resembles earlier bubbles, and analysts expect global profit growth to halve next year.
- A Federal Reserve governor again called for a half-point rate rise in December. The dollar is back at its April 2025 level.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Nvidia data-center revenue: up 110% from a year earlier.
- Nvidia margin guide: 71% to 72%, down from 74.8%.
- Anthropic: about $61 billion planned share sale, plus a $26 billion private placement.
- ECB estimate: more than $4 trillion of tech commitments kept off balance sheets.
- Global earnings growth: 16% expected in 2027, from 33.5% this year.
- S&P 500: down about 1% on Tuesday, a fourth straight loss.
- 10-year Treasury yield: above 5%.
