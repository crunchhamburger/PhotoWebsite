## Top Takeaways
- Brent climbed past $114 a barrel for the first time since May after attacks on Saudi Arabia. A drawn-out Middle East standoff keeps inflation risk high while growth worries build.
- Thursday's tech slide (Nasdaq 100 down about 3%) carried into Asia and Europe on Friday, while government bonds rallied as investors looked for safety.
- Meta's new AI agent is growing quickly, but simple math shows subscriptions would cover only a small slice of its planned spending. Its debt load has almost tripled since 2024.
- SK hynix shelved a US listing for its memory unit. Even with healthy memory prices, investors aren't rewarding new AI hardware listings right now.
- Large US public pensions now hold roughly a quarter of their US stocks in a handful of AI names. At the same time, several states are slowing data-center approvals.
- Calpers trimmed its direct Big Tech holdings by about a quarter and is shifting money into private markets.
- France's 10-year borrowing cost passed 5% for the first time since 2002, with a ratings cut expected.
- Private AI money keeps flowing: DeepSeek raised about $16B, and Anthropic is teaming up with Hg on a European consulting business.
- Washington proposed broader limits on US investment in Chinese AI, advanced memory and biotech.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Brent: about $114 a barrel, a five-month high.

- DeepSeek round: about $15.9B.
