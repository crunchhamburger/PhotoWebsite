## Top Takeaways
- The four largest cloud spenders roughly doubled their quarterly capital spending from a year ago, and three of them used up more cash than they brought in. Together they lost close to $900bn of market value after reporting.
- Amazon's cloud unit kept growing fast, but record spending pushed its free cash flow deeply negative, and its holiday outlook disappointed.
- Microsoft still produced plenty of cash despite heavy spending, and its shares held up far better than peers'.
- Lenders are pulling back from GPU-rental companies. CoreWeave's bonds are under stress, and Nscale's IPO depends on a large Anthropic contract that is not yet financed.
- Intel's outlook missed badly because AI data centers are absorbing the world's memory supply, leaving PC makers short of parts.
- The H100 rental index fell about 7% over the week, undoing Tuesday's spike.
- Exxon and Chevron posted large profits as diesel prices more than doubled since the start of the Iran war.
- The Bank of Japan held rates but said inflation has reached its goal, opening the door to a December hike.
- Binance paused withdrawals after the largest crypto theft on record; stablecoin issuers froze about half of the stolen funds.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Big Tech quarterly capex: about $207bn, versus about $113bn a year ago.
- Free cash flow at Google, Meta and Amazon combined: about –$45bn for the quarter.
- Market value lost after earnings: about $894bn.
- Global PC shipments: down about 21% in the third quarter.
- Intel's guided gross margin: 37%.
- H100 rental index: about $2.92 an hour, down about 7% in a week.
- Wholesale diesel: about $220 a barrel.
- Bank of Japan policy rate: 1.25%.
- US 10-year Treasury yield: about 4.98%, a one-month low.
