## Top Takeaways
- Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive on Thursday. Finance chief Colette Kress is interim CEO, and she is known inside the company for wanting tighter limits on the guarantees and investments Nvidia extends to its customers.
- Nvidia fell sharply during the session and closed about 4% lower, but the broad market barely moved. CoreWeave, Iren and Nebius dropped while AMD and Broadcom rose, a sign investors are sorting AI companies by how much they relied on Nvidia's money.
- Nvidia's financial support for the sector is very large: well over $100B of equity investments, plus credit support for OpenAI and promises to buy unsold cloud capacity. Smaller cloud providers borrow against those promises, so a slowdown reaches them first.
- Nscale, a data-center company preparing an IPO, disclosed that it needs more funding to meet next year's commitments tied to a large Anthropic contract.
- Huang still owns roughly $135B of Nvidia stock, and uncertainty about whether he sells hangs over the shares.
- Treasury yields had their biggest weekly rise since April 2025 after a hot inflation report, and markets expect a Fed rate increase in December. Higher rates make debt-funded AI projects costlier.
- The IEA says the oil market moved into surplus in October for the first time since the US-Iran war began, and Brent has fallen steeply this month.
- ByteDance is repurchasing shares at a higher valuation than last year. Microsoft's two-day 365 outage drew regulatory attention in the UK.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Nvidia: about 4% lower Thursday after a 14% intraday drop; about 31% below its October record.
- Nvidia equity investments at end-July: $124B; OpenAI credit support: up to $105B.
- Nscale: nearly $1.7B net loss on $307M of revenue over nine months.
- 10-year Treasury yield: 5.17% Friday, up 0.31 points on the week.
- Oil: a surplus of almost 2M barrels a day in October; Brent down 18% this month.
- ByteDance buyback valuation: about $400B, up 20% from last year.
- US existing home sales: 3.75M, the slowest pace since 2010.
