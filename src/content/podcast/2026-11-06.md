## Top Takeaways
- US stocks had their worst day since spring 2025. Large-cap tech fell more than 6%, and about $3.2tn of US market value disappeared in one session.
- The cause was the midterms: investors now expect slower approval of new data centers and less AI hardware spending.
- Memory and storage makers fell furthest, 17–22% in a day, since they rely most on capacity that is not yet built.
- Asia followed on Friday. Japan's main index is now in a bear market, and Korea briefly halted trading after a drop of more than 8%.
- October hiring came in at more than double forecasts, and core inflation is still well above target. That keeps a December rate rise in play and leaves no Fed cushion for tech.
- Anthropic's IPO still has eager buyers, but a valuation of $1.5tn or more looks harder to reach after the sell-off.
- Congress called Nvidia's chief in for a closed briefing on a reported spying case involving a big Asian chip buyer.
- Mediators are pushing for a short-term US–Iran deal to reopen Hormuz. A deal would ease oil prices.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- US market value lost Thursday: about $3.2tn. Nvidia alone lost over $400bn.
- S&P 500 −4.4%, Nasdaq 100 −6.4%.
- Micron −17%, Sandisk −22%.
- Nikkei: more than 20% below its June peak.
- October payrolls: +165k, against about 65k expected. Unemployment: 4.1%.
- Core PCE inflation: 3.6% a year.
- Anthropic's last private valuation: $965bn.
