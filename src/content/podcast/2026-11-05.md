## Top Takeaways
- US stocks had one of their worst sessions in a year and a half. The Nasdaq fell into correction territory, and semiconductor shares dropped about a tenth in a single day as investors bet on a slower AI build-out.
- Memory, storage and GPU makers fell hardest, because they depend on data centers that have not yet been approved.
- Debt markets turned on the specialist AI cloud providers. CoreWeave's bonds and credit insurance priced in sharply more risk.
- The trigger was the election. Governors who back pausing data centers won three states that together hold over 40% of planned US capacity. Projects already approved continue, so the risk is to future capacity.
- Microsoft spent about $5B less than planned last quarter because rented capacity came late, and it passed on a large Texas expansion with Nscale. Smaller providers carry that delivery risk.
- Crude fell below $100 on hopes of an end to the Iran war, and US 10-year yields hit a two-month low. The Bank of England went the other way with its first rate increase since 2023.
- Beijing vetoed SoftBank's purchase of ABB's robotics arm on security grounds.
- OpenAI has caught up with Anthropic in business spending but is raising less money than it hoped.
- Palo Alto Networks is negotiating a roughly $500M deal to secure the logins that AI agents use.
- GPU rental prices rose nearly a fifth in a week, so near-term demand for computing power is still strong.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Semiconductor index: down roughly 10% on the day, more than a third below its October peak.
- Share of planned US data-center capacity in TX, GA and OH: over 40%.
- 2027 US build-out seen at risk by one prominent investor: 25–35%.
- Microsoft capex shortfall from late third-party capacity: about $5B.
- Brent crude: in the mid-$90s, a two-month low.
- Bank of England policy rate: 4%.
- SoftBank–ABB deal blocked: $5.4B.
- H100 rental index: about $3.52 an hour, up about 19% in a week.
