## Top Takeaways
- The US now requires export licences for advanced chips going to Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore and Brazil. The rule is meant to stop Chinese firms renting Nvidia capacity there, though American tech companies can apply for exceptions.
- Federal agencies confirmed a criminal investigation, open since 2024, into Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer. Nobody has been charged, and Nvidia's board is reportedly not weighing a return for Jensen Huang.
- Nvidia reports after the close under interim chief Colette Kress. Investors are watching profit margins as memory costs rise, and whether she scales back the financing Nvidia gave its customers. This episode was written before the results.
- A Bank of America survey shows fund managers cutting tech holdings at the fastest pace in two decades. A majority now expect a large cloud company to trim its spending plans within six months.
- Anthropic has committed more than $900 billion to data centers and chips it controls, so it can rely less on rented cloud. The bills arrive before the capacity does.
- Banks are starting to sell data-center loans, some below face value, and Amazon sold most of 13 data-center buildings to a Blackstone trust and leased them back. Funding for the build-out is moving from bank balance sheets to bonds and private capital.
- All of OpenAI's planned new capacity is in states whose governors favor a pause on data centers.
