## Top Takeaways
- Jensen Huang is exploring a comeback as Nvidia's chief executive days after stepping down. The Treasury secretary wants an outside hire and has asked the board to account for the company's export compliance within ten days.
- Nvidia's interim leadership is expected to scale back the investments and guarantees it gave to AI start-ups and smaller cloud providers. Those companies lose a cheap source of funding just as interest rates rise.
- Tech shares were headed lower for a third session, and Korean memory makers fell hard overnight. CoreWeave dropped before the open even after a strong quarter, a sign that politics and financing, not demand, are driving prices.
- The sell-off has reached the companies that build and power data centers. They have given back close to half of the prior year's gains, despite record order books that some analysts think are padded by duplicate orders.
- Anthropic's IPO is due to price this week at a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion. Interest is strong, but this year's largest listings have mostly lost money, and some funds may sell chip stocks to make room.
- Washington plans to give Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic favored-exporter status while tightening limits on chips reaching China. Chinese AI developers fell sharply in Hong Kong.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Nvidia: $573B of market value lost last week, the largest weekly loss on record for a company.
- Data-center construction stocks: $4.2 trillion lost since June.
- Caterpillar: 44% below its June 30 high, with a record $78B backlog.
- Anthropic: revenue run rate of about $81B; last private valuation $965B.
- Ten largest US IPOs of 2026: down 20.5% on average from offer.
- 10-year Treasury yield: about 5.1%.
- Empire State manufacturing index: 7.8, against about 18 expected.
