## Top Takeaways
- Anthropic made its IPO filing public. It now earns an operating profit, and its compute cost per dollar of sales has dropped sharply, which weakens the idea that AI labs can never pay for themselves.
- Most of Anthropic's very large, long-dated compute contracts can't be cancelled, so they turn into years of booked business for Google, Amazon and the Broadcom chip chain. The SpaceX contract is the exception: either side can walk away on short notice.
- Buyers of the IPO get very little say. The founders and a trust control the votes and the board.
- Meta plans to fund AI by selling up to $95bn of new stock. Some of its chip suppliers may buy part of it, which links their sales to Meta's ability to keep raising money.
- SpaceX and Broadcom are moving AI chips into leasing vehicles backed by private lenders. That keeps chip orders flowing but shifts the risk of fast-ageing hardware onto those lenders.
- Two chipmakers account for about a third of this quarter's expected S&P 500 profit growth, so index earnings now depend heavily on chip and memory prices.
- Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts to about a quarter of its staff to protect cash flow while AI spending stays high.
- DoorDash is close to a deal for Instacart, which would face antitrust questions.
- Brazil's currency had its worst day in decades after a surprise election result.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Anthropic first-half revenue: about $16bn, up from about $1bn.
- Anthropic compute cost per $1 of revenue: from over $2.50 to under $0.50.
- Anthropic compute commitments: roughly $650bn–$800bn, depending on what's counted.
- Meta planned stock sale: up to $95bn.
- SpaceX chip leases via Apollo: $40bn.
- Microsoft staff offered buyouts: about 60,000.
- Brazilian real: down 8% in a day.
