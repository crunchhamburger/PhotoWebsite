## Top Takeaways
- Meta plans to raise up to $95bn by selling new shares, split between an immediate sale and a longer programme starting next year. Its AI spending has grown faster than its cash flow, so existing owners will be diluted.
- Three of Meta's chip suppliers may buy part of that stock. It supports their order books but ties their sales to Meta's ability to keep raising money.
- SpaceX lined up $40bn of private credit for AI servers, another sign that AI spending is moving to new sources of funding.
- Tech stocks have slipped more than 5% over the past month, led by chipmakers.
- Moody's left France's rating unchanged, and French borrowing costs fell back below 5%.
- Oil eased from a five-month high as talk of a US–Iran deal returned.
- The Fed, ECB and Bank of Japan all meet this week, with markets expecting more US rate rises next year.
- A non-profit hospital group agreed to buy Surgery Partners for about $5.7bn, below an earlier failed buyout price.

## Numbers Worth Remembering
- Meta's planned stock sale: up to $95bn.
- Meta's capital spending this year: up to about $145bn.
- SpaceX AI-server financing: $40bn over five years.
- French 10-year yield: about 4.9%.
- Brent crude: just under $115 a barrel.
- Fed hikes priced by end-2027: two to three.
