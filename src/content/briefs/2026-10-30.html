Article Takeaways

Big Tech lost nearly $900bn because AI spending has started to eat its cash. Q3 capex at Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and Alphabet hit $207bn (vs $113bn a year ago); Q4 guides add another $232bn, so 2026 lands near ~$740bn.

  • Google, Meta and Amazon together burned $45.2bn of free cash in Q3, against $36.4bn of positive FCF a year earlier.
  • Amazon FCF −$33.5bn on record $75.8bn capex; AWS +39% to $46bn, but revenue missed and the holiday guide was weak. Meta FCF −$2.1bn, its first cash burn since the 2012 IPO; FY capex guide $145bn.
  • Microsoft is the outlier: $45.2bn of capex but +$18.6bn FCF, and the market rewarded it.
  • The catch: funding is moving to equity and debt. Meta plans $95bn of share sales, Alphabet ~$85bn. CoreWeave's debt is partly distressed and Musk says debt markets are largely closed to neoclouds.
  • Bullish: chip and memory suppliers still get the spend. Bearish: levered neoclouds and "capex at any cost" stocks (inference). (FT)

Intel fell ~22% because the memory shortage is now crushing PC demand. Q4 revenue guide $15–16bn vs ~$17bn expected; gross margin guided to 37%, far below forecasts, on rising memory costs.

  • Global PC shipments fell 21% in Q3 (Counterpoint) as data-center buyers outbid everyone else for parts. CFO sees the slowdown lasting at least into H1 2027.
  • Data center and AI revenue $7.3bn, +78% y/y, on CPU demand for AI. Q3 revenue $16.5bn, in line.
  • The stock was +140% YTD before this but already −20% in the past month. An explosion at its Israel plant also hurt results.
  • Read-through: PC-exposed chips and OEMs are the losers in the memory squeeze; watch Apple's guide on Monday. Memory makers keep pricing power (inference). (FT)

Nscale wants a $35bn IPO, but its key project has no secured financing. Anthropic's $45bn compute deal for the first West Virginia building is over 40% of Nscale's $103bn backlog.

  • Anthropic can walk if building one isn't financed by a deadline. Microsoft exited Nscale's UK Essex deal (~$3bn of backlog) this month because power isn't available until the 2030s.
  • H1 2026: $1bn net loss on $141mn of revenue. EY puts phase one (three buildings, 2GW) at $69.2bn.
  • Only ~400 of 2,250 acres under development; air permit pending, 10 stormwater violation notices, local opposition.
  • Read-through: the neocloud IPO window looks shaky after the hyperscaler sell-off; compute backlogs carry real counterparty and financing risk (inference). (FT)

Exxon and Chevron made $25.3bn in Q3 as diesel hit $220/bbl. That's more than double pre-war (February) levels; US retail diesel is over $6, +70% y/y.

  • Exxon net income $15.4bn, its best since 2022; refining ($7.2bn) beat upstream for the first time in years. Chevron $9.9bn, −17% q/q on a $1.6bn inventory timing hit.
  • About 10% of global refining capacity has been offline over the past year (Dallas Fed). Middle East product exports are under 25% of pre-conflict levels.
  • Payouts: Exxon dividend +~4% to $1.07, $5bn/quarter buybacks continue in 2027; Chevron dividend flat, $2.5bn buyback.
  • Neither plans new refining capacity beyond existing projects, so tightness lasts through 2027 (Chevron CFO). Bad for airlines, transport and the inflation fight; a midterm headwind for Trump (inference). (FT)

The Bank of Japan says inflation is finally at 2%, setting up a December hike. It held at 1.25% on an 8–1 vote (Takata wanted 1.5%).

  • Ueda avoided a formal "achieved" declaration but raised core-core forecasts, above target through FY2028.
  • Markets expect +25bp in December. The policy rate sits ~1–1.5pp below ex-energy inflation (Deutsche Bank).
  • ETF sales sped up to ~¥10bn/day from a ~$250bn book.
  • Supports a firmer yen and higher JGB yields; mild headwind for carry trades (inference). (FT)

Binance froze withdrawals after a record ~$6.3bn hack. TRM Labs links it to North Korea's TraderTraitor group; entry came through a compromised engineer's laptop.

  • About $3bn frozen (Tether ~$1.5bn, Circle $800mn); Elliptic says ~$3.3bn was already swapped into unfreezable assets like ETH.
  • Binance has reserved over $6.34bn to cover losses and expects withdrawals back within 48 hours. Bitcoin dipped ~4%, then recovered above $78,000.
  • Read-through: favors regulated venues and third-party custody; stablecoin issuers' freeze powers are now a feature (inference). (FT)

The Through-Line

The AI build-out has reached the point where it costs more cash than the biggest companies generate, so the market is sorting winners by who can fund it. Microsoft, with positive FCF, is spared; Alphabet, Amazon and Meta are selling stock; levered neoclouds like CoreWeave and Nscale face shut funding windows. Meanwhile the same demand is starving PCs of memory (Intel) and keeps memory pricing strong (inference).

Top Takeaways

  1. Alphabet, Amazon and Meta burned a combined $45.2bn of free cash in Q3 as hyperscaler capex hit $207bn, and Big Tech lost about $894bn of value. Investors now price AI spending by whether operating cash covers it, and the funding gap is being filled with share sales (Meta $95bn, Alphabet ~$85bn) that dilute holders. (GOOGL −12.6% to −13.2% Thu, its worst day on record)
  2. Amazon posted −$33.5bn of FCF on record $75.8bn capex and missed on revenue, even as AWS grew 39% to $46bn. Cloud demand is fine; the issue is that capex is growing faster than the cash it returns, so a weak holiday guide had nowhere to hide. (AMZN −4.25% Thu close, about −7% after hours)
  3. Microsoft is the one hyperscaler still producing cash, +$18.6bn FCF on $45.2bn of capex, while offering buyouts to ~60,000 staff. Capital discipline is now what the market rewards, which pressures peers to slow spending or fund it externally. (MSFT slightly down)
  4. CoreWeave's debt is partly distressed ahead of next month's earnings, and Musk says debt markets are largely closed to neoclouds. Neoclouds borrow against customer contracts to buy GPUs, so tighter credit hits them first and hardest. (CRWV −47% over six months)
  5. Intel guided Q4 revenue to $15–16bn vs ~$17bn expected and gross margin to 37%, because data-center buyers are outbidding PC makers for memory and PC shipments fell 21%. The memory squeeze is now shifting profit from PC chips to memory makers, and Apple reports Monday. (INTC about −22% after hours)
  6. Nscale is targeting a $35bn IPO, yet over 40% of its $103bn backlog is one Anthropic deal that Anthropic can exit if building one isn't financed. Backlog that depends on unsecured financing and pending permits is weaker than the headline, and the sell-off may reset neocloud valuations. (no public ticker)
  7. Exxon and Chevron earned $25.3bn in Q3 as wholesale diesel reached $220/bbl, with ~10% of global refining offline and Middle East product exports below a quarter of pre-war levels. With no new capacity planned, refiners keep the margin through 2027 while diesel feeds inflation. (XOM about +2% premarket; CVX +1–2% premarket)
  8. The BoJ held at 1.25% but said underlying inflation is now consistent with 2%, and markets price a December hike. Higher Japanese rates pull capital home and lift JGB yields, a headwind for yen-funded trades. (Nikkei −2.1% to ~−3% Fri, led by chips)
  9. Binance halted withdrawals after a ~$6.3bn hack tied to North Korea, the largest crypto theft on record; about $3bn was frozen by Tether and Circle. Issuer freeze powers limited the damage, which strengthens the case for regulated stablecoins (inference). (CRCL; Binance has no public ticker)
  10. The H100 rental index fell to $2.92/hr, −7.30% w/w, its lowest since ~Oct 20, on the same week hyperscalers' cash burn hit the stocks. A cooling spot price alongside funding stress for neoclouds suggests GPU rental supply is loosening at the margin (inference). (Ornn H100 index; no single ticker)
