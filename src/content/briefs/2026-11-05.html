Article Takeaways

The AI trade broke on Thursday: Nasdaq down ~5% and in correction, chips down ~10%. By ~2:10pm ET the Nasdaq was −4.7%, the S&P 500 −3.6% and the Dow −2.9% (−1,530 pts). The S&P was heading for its worst day since the April 2025 tariff shock.

  • Chips took the worst of it: SOX −9% to −11%, more than 38% below its Oct 6 high and set for its second-biggest one-day drop this century. Nvidia −8.6% (now negative YTD), AMD −15%, Micron −14%, Sandisk −18%, Nebius −14% to −18%, CoreWeave −12%.
  • Credit cracked too. CoreWeave's 2032 bonds traded as low as 74.75 cents, and its 5-year CDS hit 1,262bp (+121bp on the day).
  • Wednesday's case that hyperscalers win from lower capex fell apart. By late morning staples, healthcare and utilities were falling too, so "sell AI" became "sell everything".
  • Forced selling made it worse: leveraged ETFs, multi-strat funds −315bp week on week (Goldman), and retail pulling $382mn from single stocks (Vanda).
  • Bullish: Treasurys (10-year ~4.84%, lowest since Sep 9) and European bonds. Bearish: GPUs, memory and storage, optics, and leveraged neoclouds. (WSJ, FT)

Voters just put 25–35% of next year's US data-center build at risk. Democrats who ran on pausing data centers won in Texas, Georgia and Ohio. Evercore says those three states hold more than 40% of planned US capacity.

  • Local measures passed almost everywhere: 43 of 44 (The Information) or 38 (FT), many with 70%+. Texas's governor-elect wants construction paused until new rules are written.
  • Brad Gerstner puts 25–35% of the 2027 build-out at risk. OpenAI leaders discussed whether to reset next year's capacity plans.
  • Federal risk is rising too. An AI-safety PAC network expects a sweeping frontier-AI bill by spring. Incoming senators want a frontier-AI pause (El-Sayed), a two-year moratorium (Andrews) and an end to AI tax breaks (Brown).
  • The catch: projects already approved keep going. The hit is to future capacity, so permitted sites and power gain scarcity value (inference).
  • Bullish: owners of already-permitted power and sites. Bearish: GPU, memory and power-equipment suppliers, and developers with Texas, Georgia or Ohio pipelines. (WSJ, The Information, FT)

Microsoft is walking away from third-party capacity that shows up late. Q3 capex came in ~$5B below plan because rented capacity wasn't ready, and Iren is one of the providers behind it.

  • Iren's $9.7B, five-year contract at Childress, Texas was supposed to ramp through 2026 but has slipped. Its debt costs keep running while revenue moves out.
  • Microsoft also declined an option (~500MW, Nscale says) to more than double its Nscale lease in Ward County, Texas. It had already exited a $3B lease at Nscale's Essex site.
  • Nscale is preparing a $35bn IPO with a $1bn H1 net loss on $141mn revenue, against a claimed $103bn backlog.
  • Bearish: single-customer neoclouds and contract-backed data-center debt. Bullish for Microsoft's free cash flow, since slips mean less capex (inference). (The Information, FT)

Oil fell below $100 as investors bet the new Congress will end the Iran war. Brent fell 8.7% Wednesday and was $94.23 in early London Thursday, down more than 19% since late last week.

  • VP Vance blamed the war for the midterm loss, and Schumer says the Senate majority will work to end it.
  • Gilts had their biggest one-day rally since May (10-year −13bp to 5.16%).
  • The catch: without an actual peace deal, Natixis expects Brent back above $100. It bounced to ~$97.7 (+3.2%) by the US afternoon.
  • Bullish: bonds and consumers. Bearish: upstream producers. (FT, WSJ)

The Bank of England raised rates for the first time since 2023, to 4%. The vote was 8–1, and Catherine Mann wanted 50bp.

  • Inflation is forecast at 5% in Q2 2027, and it doesn't return to 2% until Q3 2028.
  • Odds of a December hike jumped to 55% from ~20%. Bailey says more is likely unless the Middle East conflict ends.
  • Bearish: UK mortgage borrowers and domestic cyclicals. (FT)

China blocked SoftBank's $5.4B purchase of ABB's robotics unit. China is the unit's biggest market, so Beijing had a veto, and it used it on security grounds. The review looked at supply chains, IP and robot data that could train AI models.

  • It wrecks Son's physical-AI plan. SoftBank is now down 50%+ from its June high, over $140B of value gone, and −6.7% Thursday.
  • Pressures on SoftBank keep piling up: $64B in OpenAI as Anthropic pulls ahead, more debt as rates rise, and a delayed listing for its AI-infrastructure unit.
  • Bearish: SoftBank, ABB's breakup plan, and other cross-border deals that depend on China revenue (e.g. Anglo-Teck) (inference). (WSJ, FT)

OpenAI caught up with Anthropic in enterprise, but its funding round is falling short. Brockman said the first half was rough. Q3 brought a revenue inflection, and enterprise revenue now tops consumer.

  • OpenRouter data show enterprise spend roughly even between the two, from ~25/75 in Anthropic's favor in January.
  • The catch: the round targeted $30B at $1.4T and has raised less than half. It's now $20B at $1.25T, and the IPO has moved to 2027.
  • Bearish: Anthropic's IPO story at the margin, and SoftBank's OpenAI mark (inference). (WSJ)

Palo Alto Networks is buying security for AI agents. It is in talks to pay ~$500M for Clutch Security, which tracks the credentials AI agents use. That would be its fifth deal this year, extending the $25B CyberArk deal to machine identities.

  • Workday, Okta and SailPoint have each bought in this niche since June.
  • Bullish: the startups still independent in AI-agent identity. (The Information)

The Through-Line

The market has started pricing a ceiling on AI capacity, not a lack of demand. Voters limited where data centers can go, Microsoft is dropping capacity that arrives late, and the weakest borrowers (CoreWeave, Iren, Nscale) are where the stress shows first. Demand still looks strong: H100 rents are up ~19% in a week. That leaves the risk with suppliers selling into future builds and with neocloud debt, while permitted capacity gains value (inference).

Top Takeaways

  1. US stocks had their worst day since April 2025 as investors priced a slower data-center build after the Democratic sweep, with the Nasdaq −4.7% and in correction and the SOX −9% to −11%. The selling also came from leveraged ETFs, de-grossing multi-strat funds and retail, which pushed "sell AI" into defensives too. Nvidia is now negative for the year. (NVDA −8.6%)
  2. AMD, Micron and Sandisk fell 14–18% because they sell into future data-center builds. If permits for 2027 projects stall, orders for GPUs, HBM and flash get pushed out before suppliers can cut capacity. Sandisk is now 60% below its June high. (AMD −15.4%; MU −13.7%; SNDK −18%)
  3. CoreWeave's 5-year CDS jumped 121bp to 1,262bp and its 2032 bonds hit 74.75 cents because neoclouds fund new sites with debt against future contracts. Policy risk on new sites plus wider spreads raises their cost of capital just when they need to refinance. (CRWV −12.1%)
  4. Democrats who promised to pause data centers won Texas, Georgia and Ohio, which hold more than 40% of planned US capacity, and Gerstner puts 25–35% of the 2027 build at risk. Permits and grid hookups are set by states and towns, so the bottleneck for AI capex is now political, not chips or money. (NBIS −14% to −18%)
  5. Microsoft cut Q3 capex ~$5B below plan because rented third-party capacity, including Iren's $9.7B Texas contract, wasn't ready, and it declined a ~500MW expansion with Nscale. A buyer with its own land and balance sheet can drop late suppliers, which shifts execution risk onto neoclouds that rely on one customer. (MSFT −3.6%; IREN: today's move not found)
  6. Brent fell 8.7% Wednesday and sat below $100 Thursday on bets that a Democratic Congress will push to end the Iran war, which pulled the 10-year to ~4.84%, its lowest since Sep 9. Lower energy prices ease inflation and the Fed's hike case: December hike odds fell to 70.1% from 82.7%. Natixis warns oil returns above $100 without a real peace deal. (no single ticker; Brent ~$97.7 by the US afternoon)
  7. The Bank of England raised rates 25bp to 4%, its first hike since 2023, because it sees inflation peaking near 5% in Q2 2027 on energy costs from the Middle East conflict. December odds jumped to 55% from ~20%, which is bad news for UK mortgage borrowers. (no single ticker; GBP $1.348)
  8. China blocked SoftBank's $5.4B purchase of ABB's robotics unit on security grounds, because Beijing wants China-built robotics know-how and robot data kept away from rivals such as Japan. It removes the core of Son's physical-AI plan just as SoftBank's OpenAI bet weakens. (9984.T −6.7%)
  9. OpenAI has raised less than half of a $30B round and cut the target to $20B at $1.25T, even though Q3 coding products pulled its enterprise spend level with Anthropic's. Investors are pricing AI labs more cautiously after the backlash, and that also pressures Anthropic's IPO price and SoftBank's mark (inference). (no public ticker)
