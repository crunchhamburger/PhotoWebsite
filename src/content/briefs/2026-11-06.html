Article Takeaways

The midterm AI rout erased $3.2tn in one day, and memory took the worst of it. On Thursday the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4% and the S&P 500 fell 4.4%, the S&P's worst day since April 2025. By FT's count from FactSet data, the S&P 1500's $3.2tn loss was the biggest one-day drop since about 2000.

  • Memory and storage fell hardest: Sandisk −22%, Micron −17%, Western Digital −17%. Broadcom fell 10%, Qualcomm 9.5% and Nvidia 9.3%. Nvidia alone lost more than $400bn of market value.
  • The fear is that the new Democratic Congress slows data-center building. One senator-elect wants to pause frontier AI, and another proposed a two-year ban on new data centers.
  • The selling went beyond tech. Hedge funds cut positions fast and dip-buyers stayed in cash. Caterpillar (−6.4%) and Walmart (−1.1%) fell too. Tesla fell 10.8% and SpaceX 9.1% after Democrats won the Texas governor's race and a Texas Senate seat.
  • Bearish: the most crowded AI-capex trades (memory, storage, AI semis). Relative winners: defensives and non-US markets (inference). (FT)

Asia followed: Japan is now in a bear market and Korea halted trading. The Nikkei 225 and Topix each fell about 6%. The Nikkei is now more than 20% below its June peak.

  • The Kospi fell more than 8% intraday, which set off a 20-minute halt. It closed −6% and is more than 40% below its June peak.
  • SK Hynix −10.4%, Samsung −6.4%. SoftBank fell about 11% and is now about 60% below its June high. Memory maker Kioxia was also hit hard.
  • Investors bought Japanese government bonds for safety, and the 10-year yield fell 14bp to 2.63%.
  • Counterpoint: one Asia fund manager expects hyperscalers to build in other states or countries rather than cut capex. (FT)

Jobs came in far above forecasts, so a December Fed hike stays on the table. October payrolls rose 165k against a 65k consensus, and unemployment edged down to 4.1%.

  • August and September were revised up by a combined 84k. The 3-month average is now 137k, up from 28k in the prior three months.
  • Professional services added 70k, against a 15k monthly average over the past year. Healthcare, transport and manufacturing also grew.
  • Core PCE is 3.6% y/y, so inflation is the binding problem. Jefferies called a December hike "almost a lock". FT's Fed desk frames it the same way.
  • The 2-year yield briefly rose 6bp to 4.53%, then yields and the dollar ended roughly flat. US stock futures opened higher Friday: Nasdaq 100 +1.6%, S&P 500 +0.3%.
  • Bearish for long-duration tech: there is no Fed relief after the rout (inference). (FT)

Crossover funds still want the Anthropic IPO, but the $1.5tn target looks harder. XN told investors Thursday night, after the rout, that it plans to be a large buyer in the IPO.

  • XN's stake was worth about $5.3bn at the May round, which valued Anthropic at $965bn. The IPO was expected as soon as this month at $1.5tn or more.
  • Coatue expects its stake to be worth more than $13bn at a high-end price. Early holders can sell 8% in the first three months, with full release after a year.
  • The catch: the political shift, including in Texas, hits the AI growth story behind the valuation. Watch for a delay or a lower price (inference). (FT)

Congress wants Jensen Huang in a closed-door briefing on Chinese spying. Three Republican House committee chairs asked him to attend on Nov 18. This follows a report that US intelligence believes a Chinese operation targeted him through an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore cloud provider that has bought billions of dollars of Nvidia chips.

  • Nvidia's own inspections at Megaspeed reportedly found no chips diverted to China. Nvidia denies sharing sensitive information.
  • This adds political and export-control headline risk to Nvidia in the same week as the rout. Southeast Asian GPU buyers face more scrutiny (inference). (FT)

Mediators are pushing for an interim US–Iran deal before Trump can escalate. The Qatar-led proposal: Iran reopens Hormuz, and in return the US lifts its port blockade, allows Iranian oil exports and releases some frozen assets.

  • Sticking points: the order of steps, and Iran's 440kg stockpile of highly enriched uranium, which the US wants moved offshore.
  • Iran's Supreme Leader rejects concessions, and Trump has weighed strikes on Iranian infrastructure. VP Vance blamed the war for the Republican losses.
  • A deal would push oil and tanker rates down. A stall raises the risk of escalation and an oil spike (inference). (FT)

The Through-Line

The market is repricing the AI build-out for politics and rates at once. Voters threatened the data-center pipeline, crowded memory and semis trades unwound worldwide, and a hot jobs report removed the hope of a Fed rescue. Private AI valuations come next: the Anthropic IPO is the first big test of whether private marks hold up (inference).
