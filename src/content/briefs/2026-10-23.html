Friday, Oct 23, 2026 · FT, WSJ, The Information, prices

Article Takeaways

Oil broke $114 while the AI selloff rolled into Asia and Europe. Brent hit $114.72 on Friday, its first time above $114 since May, after settling +4% at $113.17 Thursday on Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia.

  • Thursday: Nasdaq 100 −2.9% after Meta's capex forecast, S&P 500 −1.2%. Treasuries had their best day since 2023 (10-year −0.19pp); on Friday the 10-year was back up to 4.98%.
  • Asia swung hard intraday: the Kospi fell as much as 4% and the Nikkei as much as 2%. Europe's Stoxx 600 fell another 0.6% after −1% Thursday.
  • The US UN ambassador told the FT a US–Iran stalemate could last another year, so hopes for a quick end are fading.
  • The catch: Deutsche Bank says the bid for JGBs isn't Japanese money coming home yet. The haven trade is about hyperscaler capex and the debt that funds it.
  • Bullish: energy producers, haven bonds. Bearish: long-duration AI and chip stocks, airlines, European periphery debt. (FT)

SK hynix scrapped its US memory-unit listing as AI stocks wobbled. It dropped plans to list the unit in the US. Seoul shares slid at first, then recovered.

  • MU, INTC and CRWV edged up premarket after Thursday's rout. Nasdaq Composite −2.6% on Oct 22.
  • Western Digital beat on AI storage demand (EPS $4.64, revenue $4.38B) but was still −0.9% premarket.
  • SpaceX rose on a report it wants 80,000+ US cell sites to build a wireless carrier. AT&T and Verizon each fell 3%+ premarket.
  • Elsewhere: Kering −12.6% on a Gucci-led sales drop. Volvo Car −27% as it cuts ~6% of staff to save $500M. Moderna extended a 17.6% Thursday drop after trial details leaked.
  • Read-through: one more AI-memory IPO pulled, so the listing window is still shut (inference). (WSJ)

Meta's Muse is the fastest-growing AI app, but it can't pay for Meta's capex. Muse passed 10M US/Canada downloads in just over a month, about 3x ChatGPT's pace, but has only ~2.5M daily users.

  • Pricing is $20 or $100 a month. At 100M users paying $20, Muse brings in ~$24B a year, about 12% of the ~$200B capex expected for 2027.
  • Street range: Citizens ~$10B at 100M users; Raymond James' base case is 175M users and ~$50B.
  • Q3 revenue +26%, but FCF was −$2.1B and is expected at −$14.1B in Q4. Long-term debt went from $28.8B (end-2024) to $80.9B.
  • The catch: only 8% of people would trust Meta with a password, plus a $10B teen-harm charge and a $25B New Mexico judgment. Rivals with more trust (Google, Anthropic, OpenAI) could win agents (inference). (WSJ)

US public pensions are heavily concentrated in the AI trade just as politicians turn on data centers. In a WSJ analysis of 13 large funds, a 15-stock AI basket made $138B of paper gains from end-2022 to June 2026, about 57% of their US equity gains.

  • They hold ~$195B in that basket, about 27% of domestic equity, up from 12% in Q4 2022. State and local pension assets rose from $5.8T to $7.3T.
  • Exposure runs into private markets too: Texas teachers report $2.6B of private data-center exposure, and California put $500M into a vehicle financing two data-center providers.
  • Pushback: New York's July moratorium on many new data centers, Pennsylvania officials wanting a pause, and Trump saying operators must bring their own power or pay community dividends.
  • The catch: indexes are so top-heavy that selling AI costs returns. Funding ratios are back to ~86%, still below the 93% before 2007–09. (WSJ)

Calpers cut its directly held Magnificent 7 shares by 26% in H1. The $640bn fund is moving money to private equity, venture and growth equity, which its deputy CIO calls cheaper than concentrated public megacaps.

  • The S&P 500 returned ~65% in the three years to March 2026, against ~24% for the Cambridge US PE index.
  • The catch: private AI marks (OpenAI, Anthropic) are inflated too, and PE still has an exit drought.
  • Read-through: the marginal seller of megacap tech is now a big institution, and PE/VC fundraising gets a strong LP vote (inference). (FT)

France's 10-year topped 5% for the first time since 2002, and foreign holders are leaving. The spread over Bunds is ~1.6–1.7pp, the widest since 2011 and more than double January's.

  • About 55% of French debt was held abroad last year. Japanese life insurers have been cutting since 2022 as hedging costs rose.
  • Moody's is expected to downgrade France on Friday but keep it investment grade. The 2025 deficit was 5.1% of GDP.
  • France has $1T+ maturing by 2030 and plans a record ~$380B of bonds next year. Pantheon says it muddles through at a 4% deficit but gets punished at 5%.
  • On Friday the 10-year eased to 5.05%. Watch euro-area spread contagion and French bank balance sheets (inference). (WSJ, FT)

Anthropic and Hg are launching a European AI consultancy. The independent company will place AI consultants and engineers inside European businesses, starting with Hg's portfolio. It aims to be worth $10bn+ within five years.

  • Launch is due by end-2026 with London and Berlin hubs. Anthropic invests balance-sheet cash; Hg invests from its funds, including HgCapital Trust.
  • It copies the US playbook: Anthropic's "Ode" with Blackstone and H&F ($1.5bn planned), and OpenAI's deployment company with TPG, Advent, Bain and Brookfield.
  • Context: Anthropic needs new revenue to justify data-center spending before its IPO, and its Q3 growth slowed.
  • Bearish: Accenture, Capgemini and Big Four tech advisory fees. Bullish: PE software owners (inference). (FT)

DeepSeek raised ~$15.9B, more than double its target. The 106B yuan round values it above 600B yuan post-money, and it is preparing a Shanghai IPO.

  • The round is about twice its June raise. The extra cash goes to compute: more Nvidia Blackwell access and more Huawei chips for training.
  • Read-through: Chinese AI capital is deepening while US labs' funding gets harder, which adds pricing pressure on US models (inference). (The Information)

The US Treasury proposed wider limits on investing in Chinese tech. The draft would bar US investment in all AI systems (not just models above a compute threshold) and in some chip areas, including high-bandwidth memory. Hypersonics and biotech are added.

  • Some cell and gene therapies and immunotherapies would be banned outright. Cash-only in-licensing deals by Lilly, Pfizer and BMS stay exempt.
  • Public comment runs to Dec 7. The biotech lobby BIO says the rule is too broad.
  • Read-through: more US VC/PE decoupling from China AI and biotech, and more pharma deals pushed into licensing structures (inference). (The Information, FT)

Datadog offered nearly $4B for cloud security startup Upwind. That's ~80x Upwind's $50M+ ARR, richer than Google's multiple for Wiz. Upwind is closing a $300M round at ~$3.8B, so the offer carries little premium.

  • Upwind was valued at $1.5B in January, and Datadog tried to buy it for ~$1B last year. ARR has grown from ~$20M at end-2025.
  • Driver: AI agents in the cloud and AI-found exploits are pushing up security budgets. Read-through: cyber M&A multiples keep rising (inference). (The Information)

Citi finally got approval for a wholly owned China securities business. It covers brokerage and underwriting, about five years after Citi applied, and it lands ahead of the expected Trump–Xi meeting at APEC in Shenzhen next month. Visa won card approval earlier this month.

  • The catch: foreign firms have struggled to win share from state-backed banks. Expect goodwill, not near-term revenue (inference). (FT)

The Through-Line

The money behind AI is moving. Public holders are cutting back (Calpers), politicians are pushing back on data centers that pensions are already exposed to, and Meta and SK hynix are running into a closed equity window. Meanwhile private capital is still flowing to the labs (DeepSeek's $16B) and into services that turn AI into revenue (Anthropic–Hg). Oil at $114 and 5% French yields keep the cost of capital high while all of this reprices (inference).
