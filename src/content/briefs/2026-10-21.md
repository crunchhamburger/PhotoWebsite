## Article Takeaways

**Anthropic's draft S-1 shows 15x revenue growth and a $600B+ compute bill.** H1 2026 revenue was ~$16.3B vs $1.1B a year earlier; all of 2025 was $4.6B.
- H1 compute spend was ~$9B (vs $2.3B), already more than all of 2025 ($7.3B).
- H1 net loss was $80.7B, but ~$72B of that is a non-cash fair-value charge on convertible notes and other funding instruments.
- ~$450B of mostly noncancelable cloud commitments over 7–10 years: Google $111B, AWS $110B, Microsoft $31B, plus up to $86B renting Nvidia chips from Nscale and Lambda. Add >$160B for Google TPUs via Broadcom and obligations top $600B; talks under way could add up to $150B more.
- The catch: about a fifth of H1 revenue came from two customers. Hyperscaler partners also capped capacity and increasingly see Anthropic as a rival. The IPO could target ~$2T.
- Bullish: locked-in backlog for GOOGL, AMZN, MSFT, AVGO and neoclouds. Bearish: the fixed-cost stack if growth slows (inference). *(WSJ)*

**SpaceX just printed an AI-cloud quarter, and Musk wants to fold in Tesla.** Both boards set up special committees to study a merger. Musk recuses himself, no terms are agreed, and a deal needs shareholder and regulatory approval.
- A combined ~$3.5T group (SpaceX ~$2T, Tesla ~$1.5T) would be the largest merger ever.
- Q3 revenue rose 161% to $14.6B (est. $12.9B), and net profit was $4.4B. AI/cloud revenue was $7.6B, 9x a year ago, and includes xAI, Grok and data-centre rentals. Starlink did $5.2B.
- Capex was $19.6B and is guided to $35–40B in Q4. Compute was 1.7GW at end-September, more than CoreWeave's 1.5GW, and Musk targets ~13GW by end-2027.
- The catch: shares sit at $148 vs a $225 post-IPO peak, and its own AI work lags the rental business.
- Bullish: GPU and power demand. Bearish for listed neoclouds, which now face a bigger rival (inference). *(WSJ, FT)*

**Musk throttled Ukraine's Starlink, crippling some of its drones.** Starting Oct. 14, speeds on the military link were capped, limiting some strike and interceptor drones to under ~130 km/h.
- It followed anger from Musk and Trump over Ukrainian strikes on Russian diesel refineries. Rubio says the administration had no advance knowledge.
- The catch: one company controls a key piece of battlefield communications. That adds European and congressional risk for SpaceX just as it floats a Tesla deal (inference). *(WSJ)*

**Nvidia has more than $170B on the line for its own customers.** That covers up to $164.5B of guarantees and commitments plus >$7B invested in AI cloud providers.
- The biggest item is a $105B lease guarantee for OpenAI's Ohio campus. The rest: $36B of promises to buy unsold cloud capacity, $20B of pass-through leases and $3.5B of lease guarantees.
- Neoclouds and enterprises were 45% of data-centre revenue (~$40B) last quarter, more than double a year ago.
- The CFO calls the risk limited because chips can be redeployed. Each new backstop still raises the question of how organic the growth is. *(The Information)*

**Nvidia is stepping in to rescue Firmus after its IPO flopped.** It is in talks to invest hundreds of millions of dollars in the Australian neocloud, which pulled a reported $5B listing this month.
- Firmus wants ≥$2B privately. It has raised >$13B, ~$10B of it debt, and Nvidia already backstops its ~$15B purchase of 170,000+ GPUs for an Indonesian site.
- Nvidia now offers shorter credit support for a smaller cut of revenue after big neoclouds rejected its original terms.
- Read-through: this cuts near-term order risk for NVDA but adds contingent exposure (inference). *(The Information)*

**NextDC lost 44% in a day on power, not demand.** The power upgrade for its Melbourne M3 site slipped to 2027–28, and planning approvals wait on next month's state election.
- It cut its earnings forecast by more than a third and lowered revenue guidance. Market cap is now ~A$4bn, down from shares above A$16 in June.
- Elsewhere, AirTrunk paused a listing and Goodman dropped a Sydney site after local opposition.
- Bearish: build timelines across data-centre names. RBC flags community pushback as a timing risk. *(FT)*

**Nscale's backlog is 97% promises.** Of $103B of contracted value at end-August, only $2.6B was active. The CEO told staff demand is "insatiable."
- H1: $1B net loss on $141M revenue. It still aims for a NY IPO by year-end at $30–35B, vs $14.6B in March.
- The catch: Microsoft left its Loughton lease, and Anthropic can walk from the $45B West Virginia deal if Nscale misses financing or delivery deadlines.
- CoreWeave and Nebius fell close to 25% last week. *(FT)*

**More Gulf oil is flowing than before the war, but moving it costs a fortune.** Middle East flows topped 24M bbl/d in the week to Oct. 18 vs 23.3M prewar (Kpler); Hormuz is at ~82% of prewar.
- Freight from the Gulf to China is near a record $37.37/bbl, and war-risk insurance is up to 10% of hull value (from <3%).
- There have been ~30 ship attacks so far in October. JPMorgan says the problem is now transport cost, not crude shortage.
- Bullish: tankers and insurers. A de-escalation could knock Brent down sharply (inference). *(WSJ)*

**Israel planned to restart Iran strikes and shelved it only because of the U.S. midterms.** The IDF planned a multi-day campaign for the weekend of Oct. 10, larger than February's offensive, and Trump blocked it.
- The White House says no attack before Nov. 3. Sources say either a deal or an attack is coming. Israel votes Oct. 27, and Netanyahu keeps war powers as caretaker even if he loses.
- Read-through: escalation risk for crude and diesel rises after Nov. 3, so the risk premium likely holds until then (inference). *(FT)*

**Heating oil hit a record $6.14/gal, up 50% since Hormuz closed in February (EIA).** Low-income north-east households face $1.1B more in heating costs this winter.
- Half of Maine homes use heating oil. DoE put $42M of Defense Production Act money into the Constitution gas pipeline, but it comes too late for this winter.
- Bullish: Appalachian gas (EQT), pipeline sponsor Williams and diesel cracks. Adds midterm pressure for reserve releases (inference). *(FT)*

**France's 10-year yield hit 5% for the first time since 2002.** It is up >20bp this week and from 3.2% in late February. The OAT–Bund spread is ~1.6pp, a level last seen in the euro debt crisis.
- The trigger was days of riots on top of Iran-war inflation. There is little appetite for cuts before April's presidential election.
- The catch: Moody's reviews France on Friday (Aa3, negative outlook), and a downgrade is a real possibility.
- Bearish: French banks and periphery spreads. Bunds and gilts gain on a relative basis. *(FT)*

**UK inflation jumped to 3.5%, and a November hike is now almost fully priced.** Services were 3.7% and core 2.8%. Borrowing was £24.5B, £1.9B above forecast, and debt passed £3T.
- The FT estimates fiscal headroom has shrunk to ~£12B from £23.6B before the Oct. 28 Budget. The 10-year gilt is at 5.42%, near the 5.53% high.
- Hawkish for short gilts. Tax rises are likely. *(FT)*

**Private equity looked at a $100–150B Xbox buyout.** The talks were exploratory and never became negotiations; Microsoft says Xbox isn't for sale.
- Xbox revenue fell 7% to $21.8B with an adjusted margin of ~3%. ~3,200 job cuts (~20%) are planned.
- It would dwarf EA's record $55B LBO. A sale would free cash for MSFT's AI capex (inference). *(The Information)*

## The Through-Line
The AI money is committed. Anthropic has $600B+ locked in, Nscale has $103B on paper, and Nvidia is guaranteeing $170B of its customers' obligations. The risk has moved to delivery (power, permits, financing) and to whoever backstops it: NextDC's 44% drop and Firmus's rescue are that risk showing up. Meanwhile, oil-driven inflation keeps 10-year yields at 5%+ in the U.S., UK and now France, which raises the cost of every financing that build-out depends on (inference).
