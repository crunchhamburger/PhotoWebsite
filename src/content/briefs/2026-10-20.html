Tuesday, Oct 20, 2026 · FT, WSJ, The Information, prices

Article Takeaways

Credit markets are now the main gauge of AI stress. Morgan Stanley says weekly CDS trading on Oracle, SpaceX, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Broadcom and Nvidia bonds averaged more than $9bn in September, up from under $2bn in July.

  • AI-linked debt issuance is ~$466bn YTD vs $101bn at this point in 2025. The pace has slowed since a June peak.
  • Record 5y CDS this month: Meta 108bp, Nvidia 101bp. Oracle is at 322bp (144bp in January) and SpaceX at 226bp (110bp in late June).
  • CoreWeave 5y CDS is ~1,090bp. Protecting $10mn costs $1.82mn upfront plus $125k a quarter, and some of its bonds are distressed.
  • The catch: circular financing. Oracle and Alphabet supply OpenAI and Anthropic, so the weakest link can drag the rest.
  • Bearish: levered neoclouds and Oracle. Hyperscaler spreads are still tight in absolute terms (inference). (FT)

CoreWeave's debt binge has reached a reckoning. The Information's exclusive traces how CoreWeave won Blackstone back as a lender in 2023 with a multiyear, multibillion-dollar compute contract, months after Blackstone first said no.

  • Paywalled; only the headline and teaser were visible.
  • CRWV fell ~25% last week. Its lenders, Blackstone among them, face scrutiny if refinancing gets harder (inference). (The Information)

The neocloud IPO window just shut in Seoul. Korean data-centre builder Elice fell 32% on debut to W61,500, below its W90,500 IPO price. It was the Kosdaq's biggest IPO this year at ~W201bn ($151mn).

  • Demand looked hot: >W10tn in subscription deposits, retail tranche >400x covered. Retail now holds big paper losses.
  • Kospi fell 3.7% to its lowest since early April, led by SK Hynix (−4.2%) and Samsung (−3%), even after US tech bounced overnight.
  • Pattern: Firmus scrapped a $5bn IPO <2 weeks ago, CoreWeave is down more than half from its May peak, and Nscale's valuation is in question.
  • Bearish: Nscale's $35bn NY listing. Korean memory names now trade as high-beta AI proxies (inference). (FT)

Anthropic is lining up a second multibillion-dollar Nscale deal. It would roughly double its compute commitment and cover a second 460MW building at the Monarch campus in West Virginia, on terms like August's $45bn deal.

  • It comes days after Microsoft quit Nscale's UK site. OpenAI left an Nscale partnership in April.
  • Nscale lost $1bn on $141mn revenue in H1. ByteDance was 73% of its 2025 revenue.
  • The catch: not signed, permits for the on-site gas plant are pending, and Nscale had no binding financing as of mid-September. Anthropic can walk if financing misses a deadline.
  • Anthropic buys compute as delivered, so Nscale carries the buildings, chips and risk. (FT)

Anthropic will put 5% of its shares into a "public benefit" fund ahead of its IPO. The fund is an LLC, not a nonprofit. It can give to charity or pay groups hurt by AI disruption, and the share commitment rises if its value grows past a baseline.

  • With founder and employee pledges, ~20% of shares are committed to the public good.
  • The IPO could raise up to $100B at ~$2T, with a public filing expected in weeks. OpenAI's nonprofit got a 26% stake in its restructuring.
  • Musk followed with a pledge of one SpaceX share for every US child, days before SpaceX reports (inference: a PR counter). (WSJ)

DeepSeek's new Flash model undercuts OpenAI by 17–33x. V4.1-Flash-1017 beat GPT5.6 Sol on Terminal-Bench 4.0, a multi-step agent test. API output costs $1.20 per 1M tokens at peak and $0.60 off-peak, vs $20 for Sol.

  • The catch: one benchmark. It narrows the gap; it doesn't match frontier models across the board.
  • Bearish: US lab API pricing, and it revives "cheap China AI" doubts about capex, as in January 2025 (inference). (The Information)

Greg Ip: yes, the Fed can kill the AI boom. Tightening works through liquidity, not just borrowing costs. IPO, high-yield, private-credit and securitisation appetite dries up.

  • He points to 2000, when founders also said Fed hikes didn't matter. The Fed targets 2% inflation, and it has the tools if draining liquidity is the only route.
  • From the IMF meetings: policymakers worry most about high rates; tariffs are "priced in."
  • Read-through: CoreWeave, Elice and Nscale are this thesis playing out (inference). (WSJ)

Oil hit a five-month high on Hormuz. Brent traded just over $111 after Iran again claimed sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz. Shipping attacks and Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia added to it.

  • Deutsche Bank: a US–Iran deal before the midterms looks increasingly hard. Saudi Arabia postponed its FII conference.
  • Conflict: the WSJ quote widget later showed Brent at $108.24 (−1.2%), so the spike faded intraday.
  • Higher oil slows disinflation and adds to the Fed risk above (inference). (WSJ)

Stock pickers think the AI rotation is their moment. Morningstar: 70% of active managers trailed benchmarks YTD through Oct 8. Large-blend funds (~$11T) started 2026 at 28.4% AI stocks vs 36.5% for the S&P 500.

  • Vanguard Dividend Growth held no Nvidia and rose 1.9% last week as the S&P fell about as much.
  • Investors are now sorting AI names by balance sheet: Nasdaq rose 1.1% Monday while CoreWeave lagged. (WSJ)

Defence stocks are trading as a bet on a Democratic sweep. ITA fell 16% from early July to mid-October vs S&P +3.5%. Northrop is −35% and L3Harris −36%.

  • At risk: Trump's proposed $500bn boost (to $1.5tn a year), F-35, Sentinel and "Golden Dome." Missile restocking keeps bipartisan support.
  • The catch: backlogs run years and global demand is strong. Q3 earnings are next week. (FT)

Japan says it may run short of yttrium by end-2026 as China squeezes supply. Yttrium coats jet engines and chip plasma-etch chambers. Ex-China yttrium oxide is $1,150/kg, up 179x in two years, vs $9/kg inside China.

  • A US official says China meets ~two-thirds of its rare-earth commitments to the US but gives Japan the bare minimum.
  • The US is withholding C919 engines, and China's 200-Boeing order is still not done.
  • Bearish: chip-equipment and aerospace supply chains. Bullish: non-China rare-earth processors (inference). (FT)

The US sanctioned five Chinese entities tied to Google AI trade-secret theft. They include ex-Google engineer Linwei Ding's startup and four entities linked to MiraclePlus, the former YC China.

  • Enforcement now reaches Chinese VC and incubators, not just individuals. (The Information)

The Through-Line

Rates and credit are now pricing AI risk before equities do. The 10-year is ~5.14%, CDS volumes have more than quadrupled, and the weakest borrowers are paying: CoreWeave, Nscale and Elice. The labs keep their options. Anthropic signs compute deals it can walk away from, while builders carry the debt. Cheap Chinese models and pricier oil add pressure from both sides.

Top Takeaways

  1. Weekly CDS trading on Oracle, Meta, Nvidia, Alphabet and other AI borrowers jumped from under $2bn in July to over $9bn in September, and Oracle's 5y CDS has more than doubled to 322bp. Investors are hedging AI with credit because ~$466bn of AI debt was issued this year and CDS is cheaper than equity puts. Oracle looks most exposed among the big names, as a key OpenAI and Anthropic supplier whose spread has widened fastest (inference). (ORCL +1.06%)
  2. CoreWeave's 5y CDS is ~1,090bp, some of its bonds are distressed, and The Information details how its borrowing built to a "reckoning." Rising rates make its debt-funded GPU fleet harder to refinance, and lenders like Blackstone carry the risk. (CRWV −0.44% Monday)
  3. Korean AI data-centre builder Elice fell 32% on its Kosdaq debut after raising ~$151mn and pricing at the top of the range. Following Firmus pulling its $5bn IPO, it shows public buyers won't pay up for neocloud growth while rates are high. That hurts Nscale's planned $35bn listing. (Elice −32% on debut)
  4. The Kospi fell 3.7% as SK Hynix and Samsung sold off, even though US chips and memory rallied Monday (SOX +1.61%, MU +2.97%). The two names are about half the index and a crowded AI trade, so they fall harder when foreign investors cut AI risk (inference). Memory pricing hasn't changed. (000660.KS −4.2%)
  5. Anthropic is negotiating a second Nscale deal of similar size to the $45bn August deal, for another 460MW building in West Virginia, days after Microsoft left Nscale's UK site. Anthropic pays only for compute delivered and can walk if financing fails, so Nscale carries the building, chips and permit risk. (no public ticker)
  6. Anthropic will put 5% of its shares into a public benefit fund that grows with its value, bringing the total committed to the public good to ~20%, ahead of an IPO of up to $100B at ~$2T. IPO buyers face more dilution and unusual governance in exchange for exposure to a leading lab. (no public ticker)
  7. DeepSeek's V4.1-Flash-1017 beat OpenAI's GPT5.6 Sol on an agent benchmark at $0.60–1.20 per million output tokens vs $20. Cheap, capable open models push down API prices and make investors question the returns on frontier-lab capex (inference). (no public ticker)
