Article Takeaways

October CPI came in at Waller's 50bp trigger, and a December hike is now close to priced in. Core rose 0.3% m/m, broad-based (airfares +0.4%, auto insurance +0.7%), after a hot September. Waller had said that print could justify 50bp.

  • The 2-year had its biggest one-day jump in 19 months. The 10-year touched 5.021% and closed at 4.982%, up from 4.863% Monday, still below the early-October peak near 5.4%.
  • Markets treat a hike at the Dec. 9 FOMC as near-certain, with about 10% odds of 50bp. State Street's Arone expects 25bp in December and 25bp in January.
  • Nvidia fell 5.5% Tuesday after −4.3% Monday and has lost about $430B in value since the spy-probe news. The S&P 500 fell 0.4% and the Nasdaq 0.7%.
  • The catch: the AI capex boom itself keeps inflation sticky, so tech faces slower demand and higher rates at the same time.

(WSJ)

CoreWeave beat and found money outside the bond market. Q3 revenue was $3.7bn (+168% y/y), above the $3.45–3.6bn guide. The FY guide rose to $13–13.3bn, and backlog reached $132.5bn, up from ~$104bn three months earlier.

  • The CEO won't issue unsecured debt at current yields (its 9.625% 2032 bond yields ~15%). Instead: a chip-backed loan of several billion dollars at ~7–7.5% for an investment-grade customer, ~$9bn of customer prepayments in the next three months, and vendor financing.
  • Costs are heavy: net loss $709mn (from $110mn), interest expense $846mn (up to $1.15bn in Q4), and debt plus leases $66.2bn. Q3 capex was $14.5bn against $2bn of operating cash flow.
  • 2026 capex up to $39bn; 2027 could stay flat if only committed capacity is built. 1.7GW active now, target ≥8GW by 2030.
  • The catch: Needham says landlords now want a full investment-grade backstop before leasing to CoreWeave, and Moody's flagged force-majeure risk in a secured deal.
  • Bullish: customers and lenders backed by chips now carry the funding. Bearish: unsecured neocloud credit, and a flat-2027 option is a small negative for Nvidia orders (inference).

(FT; WSJ)

Anthropic's bankers may price above the range, at $1.5tn or more. The range filed Tuesday ($70–78) implied $1.26–1.41tn fully diluted. Listing is expected next week.

  • Base raise up to $64bn plus ~$10bn greenshoe. A higher price pushes total proceeds past $100bn, with Google, Nvidia and Microsoft adding $26bn at the IPO price.
  • Backers say the aim is cash for ~$900bn of planned compute spend, not the top valuation. Hopes of $2tn had already faded.
  • Morningstar puts fair value at $1.7tn, within a $500bn–$3tn range.
  • The catch: grey-market flipping. A brokerage is offering up to $500mn of allocations at 4% over the IPO price, and a VC SPV is offering $1.25bn at $1.5tn. That is after-market supply (inference).

(FT)

Anthropic added ~$10B of cloud commitments and flagged that the Lambda deal could be cancelled. Azure rises to $49.2B (from $44.9B) and Google Cloud to $121.6B (from $114.5B), through 2033.

  • Unchanged: Broadcom $161.2B, SpaceX $120.5B, Amazon $110B. Total planned cloud spend is ~$650B per the S-1.
  • Lambda missed a data-center deadline, so its $34.9B lease can be cancelled. Anthropic says it doesn't intend to cancel for now.
  • Short-term compute deals cost more and swung Q3 to a $567M operating loss from a $974M Q2 profit.
  • Hyperscalers win share. Neoclouds that miss delivery dates carry cancellation risk (inference).

(The Information)

Anthropic launched Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards, and Microsoft fell 3.5%+ intraday. It also previewed a meeting notetaker. Users can do Office- and Workspace-type work inside Claude without those subscriptions.

  • The odd part: Microsoft and Google are both IPO investors in Anthropic and earn cloud revenue from its compute spend.
  • Bearish for Microsoft 365 and Workspace seat pricing. Supports Anthropic's enterprise pitch before the IPO (inference).

(The Information)

OpenAI's revenue jumped 50% to ~$10B in Q3, but its loss still widened. Q2 growth was only 18%; Q3 2025 revenue was $3.9B.

  • Adjusted operating margin improved from −150% to −109%, but the adjusted operating loss grew to $10.9B. That excludes $2.6B of stock comp.
  • Anthropic, by contrast: $17B Q3 revenue and a $567M operating loss including stock comp.
  • ~100M paying ChatGPT users, 8.5% of weekly actives. It cut model prices for enterprise share and plans an IPO next year.
  • The catch: its latest round raised less than half of a $30B target and is still open. That keeps pressure on partners tied to its compute bills, such as SoftBank, Oracle and CoreWeave (inference).

(WSJ; same scoop in The Information)

Google's search ad growth slowed to 12% from 17%, and AI Mode is part of the reason. Search ads are a $224.5B/yr business. Meta's ad revenue is growing more than twice as fast.

  • AI Mode queries run ~3x longer and show fewer ads, which pushes up cost per click.
  • Optmyzr (21,000 accounts): Q3 conversion rate −1.4% q/q and return on ad spend −2.9% after −3.8% in Q2.
  • Six advertisers or agencies are moving budget to Meta and ChatGPT ads. Advertisers can't opt out of AI-feature ads or measure them separately.
  • The catch: this could be a Reels-style dip that recovers as AI ad formats mature.

(The Information)

SoftBank's turbine bet backfired. Its $3.7B hit was mainly cancelled reservations on more than 13GW of gas turbines, largely from GE Vernova, which ships ~20GW a year. Reservation fees are nonrefundable, typically tens of millions of dollars per GW.

  • Part of the order was for a $33B, 9.2GW gas plant in Ohio feeding an 8GW data center leased to OpenAI. Construction still starts early next year.
  • The CFO signalled a pullback and denied that SoftBank defaulted on the orders. New bonds carry a 9.75% coupon, CDS costs have surged, and the SB Energy IPO is delayed.
  • Shares are down 55%+ since June. GE Vernova says the cancellations don't affect it either way.
  • Bullish: freed turbine slots ease the bottleneck for other data-center builders. Bearish: more AI-financing retrenchment at SoftBank (inference).

(WSJ)

Investors are souring on AI energy start-ups. Oklo, X-energy, Bloom and Fervo are all off their 12-month peaks, and the selling got worse on fears that a Democratic Congress blocks data-center projects.

  • Balance sheets are fine for now: Oklo has net cash above $3bn, and Fervo has $1.9bn.
  • Holtec pulled a $900mn IPO at a $10bn valuation in September; Westinghouse's IPO is in question.
  • The catch: these sources cost more than gas and renewables, and they need binding offtakes, not letters of intent.
  • Bearish for high-cost AI power and pending nuclear IPOs. Hyperscalers favour cheaper power (inference).

(FT)

The CFTC delayed GPU rental-price futures by at least 60 days. That pushes CME's H100 and B200 contracts (Silicon Data indices) to early 2027 at the earliest. ICE and Kalshi had similar plans.

  • Concern: manipulation, given opaque rental prices and a few dominant suppliers.
  • Use case: lock in compute prices, hedge GPU depreciation, and help lenders value chip collateral.
  • This delays a hedging tool just as chip-backed lending grows (see CoreWeave), and a new revenue line for CME and ICE (inference).

(FT)

Commerce is stalling the blacklisting of Megaspeed, the Nvidia partner at the centre of the smuggling probe. State, Energy, Treasury and the Pentagon want it on the entity list. Commerce has added no new names for two months.

  • A former export-control official says Megaspeed bought $4.8bn of Nvidia chips while Commerce knew about it for months.
  • Nvidia says it block-listed Megaspeed in June 2025. Commerce says six smuggling probes have charged 17 defendants.
  • Bearish: political overhang on Nvidia, and a higher chance of tighter Southeast Asia export rules (inference).

(FT)

Tech futures are set to extend the rebound. Nasdaq 100 futures were +1.9% and S&P 500 futures +0.9%, led by CoreWeave (+17% premarket) and Nvidia (+4%). The Nasdaq 100 is already +2.8% this week after last week's 4.9% drop.

  • Drivers: CoreWeave's beat and the report of a higher Anthropic valuation. Deutsche Bank says CoreWeave eased doubts about the AI cycle.
  • Fidelity International, which took profits in October, is debating moving back into tech.
  • Sources differ on size: earlier quotes in the price notes had Nasdaq 100 futures +1.5–1.6% and CoreWeave +13–16% premarket. The gap is timing.

The Through-Line

Money is getting more expensive, and the strong AI names are raising it around the bond market. CoreWeave borrowed against chips and customer prepayments, and Anthropic leans on strategic investors and a bigger IPO, while the weak links (SoftBank's turbines, Lambda's lease, SMR start-ups, OpenAI's half-filled round) are getting cut. The H100 index fell 12% in a week even as CoreWeave's backlog grew, so watch whether compute pricing follows funding stress (inference).
