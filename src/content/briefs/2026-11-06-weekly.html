The week in one paragraph

Voters hit the AI trade where it is weakest: the land and power it needs. Tuesday's Democratic sweep put candidates who promised data-center pauses into office in Texas, Georgia and Ohio, which hold over 40% of planned US capacity. Wednesday rallied on gridlock and cheaper oil. Thursday broke: $3.2tn of US market value gone in a day, the Nasdaq 100 −6.4%, chip stocks −12.2% (their second-worst day since 1994), as hedge funds dumped crowded AI positions and retail sold early. Friday's bounce was thin, and a hot jobs report (165k vs ~60k expected) kept a December Fed hike near 78% odds. Over the weekend Jensen Huang admitted an affair with a suspected Chinese agent and Nvidia's board opened a probe, while Anthropic said it will still list this month, at a lower price. Week: Nasdaq 100 −4.9%, Nasdaq −3.4%, S&P 500 −2.7% (worst week since April 2025), Russell 2000 −2.1%, Dow −0.9%. Bonds, gold and Berkshire were the havens; oil fell ~13%.

Article Takeaways

The midterms ended the market's "blank cheque" for AI, and Thursday's rout was the biggest one-day loss of US equity value on record. US stocks lost $3.2tn on Thursday; the Nasdaq Composite alone shed $2.49tn, far above its previous worst ($1.87tn in June).

  • The Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4%, its worst day since 2020, and the chip index fell 12.2%, second only to March 16, 2020 since 1994.
  • Friday recovered less than 10% of the S&P's Thursday loss, against a ~46% average rebound after 4%+ drops since 1928.
  • What calmed Friday: Texas governor-elect Hinojosa said her moratorium covers new permits only and is temporary, so 2027 construction shouldn't stop.
  • Bearish: future-capacity suppliers (memory, storage, AI semis). Bullish: Treasurys, gold, defensives, already-permitted sites (inference). (FT, WSJ)

Data centers are now a voting issue, and the industry is already routing around it. Texas, Georgia and Ohio, over 40% of planned US capacity (Evercore ISI), elected Democrats who pledged to slow building; local bans passed almost everywhere.

  • Gerstner puts 25–35% of next year's US build-out at risk. OpenAI leaders discussed resetting 2027 capacity plans; Anthropic's filing already lists moratoriums as a risk.
  • 56% of voters said data centers were a crucial or major factor in their congressional vote (SSRS). AI investment is ~1.9% of GDP this year (Goldman).
  • Workarounds: Nvidia proposed retrofitting 130+ buildings of Apollo-owned Brightspeed (~1GW of power) with Vera Rubin servers from Q1 2027; Google committed $15bn in Malaysia.
  • Bullish: existing powered buildings and overseas sites. Bearish: greenfield developers in TX/GA/OH (inference). (WSJ, The Information)

Hedge funds were forced sellers, which turned a policy scare into a crash. October was the biggest active unwind at stock-picking funds in over a decade (Morgan Stanley); their market exposure is down ~9% since June.

  • JPMorgan's hedge fund clients lost 1.6% Thursday, the worst day since the April 2025 tariff shock; TMT funds lost 4%+, erasing most of the year's gains.
  • More than 40% of stock-picking funds were flat or negative through October. Walleye, Samlyn and Engineers Gate cut staff; Lone Pine lost 8.8% in October.
  • Retail sold ~$388mn of single stocks in Thursday's first two hours (Vanda), then partly bought back.
  • Read: positioning, not fundamentals, set Thursday's size, so the bounce can be quick but fragile (inference). (WSJ, FT)

Anthropic will still list before Thanksgiving, but likely at $1.25–1.5tn, not $2tn. It plans to raise $80–100bn; three big investors told the WSJ the price must come down, one saying closer to $1tn.

  • Run-rate revenue topped $80bn at end-October (~$81bn, up from ~$65bn in July). Cash was $146.6bn on Sep 30.
  • JPMorgan hosts an investor session Nov 13; listing is expected the week of Nov 16. XN plans to be a big buyer; a UK retail tranche is being lined up.
  • It also bought 1.7GW of Caterpillar gas generators for $3.4bn from OnePWR, the largest known power-equipment purchase by an AI lab.
  • Bullish: index "forced buyers" after listing, power-equipment makers. Bearish: every AI holder that marks to Anthropic's price (inference). (WSJ, FT, The Information)

The market is being asked to absorb a record amount of AI stock. AI-linked companies have sold or priced more than $300bn of new stock and mandatory converts this year, against $194bn for all US IPOs and follow-ons in 2025.

  • About $100bn more is queued, mostly at-the-market programs, including a proposed $60bn Meta ATM from early 2027. Alphabet had sold $12.4bn of its $40bn ATM by Sep 30.
  • Iren raised ~$3.7bn via its ATM while its stock fell ~48%, and has now cut capex.
  • CoreWeave closed Friday at $46.40, $6.40 above its IPO price, down ~50% in a month; Q3 cash burn is expected near $10.7bn. It reports Tuesday. (The Information)

Memory went from the AI trade's winner to its biggest casualty in a day. Sandisk −22%, Micron −17% and Western Digital −17% Thursday; SK Hynix −10.4% and the Kospi −6% Friday.

  • Japan's Nikkei is now in a bear market (−20%+ from June); SoftBank is ~60% off its high.
  • TSMC is drawing up plans to make 3D DRAM at Nvidia's request, which would add a fourth big memory maker over time.
  • DRAM spot softened: DDR5 16Gb −2.2% and DDR4 lines −2.4% to −3.7% on the week; only DDR5 eTT rose (+1.5%).
  • Bearish: memory makers' multiples if supply widens. Bullish: Nvidia's bargaining power over memory costs (inference). (FT, The Information)

AMD showed AI demand is still real: data centers are now 64% of sales. Q3 revenue $13.4bn (+45%) vs $13.1bn expected; data-center revenue nearly doubled to $8.5bn; Q4 guide ~$17bn vs $16.23bn.

  • The catch: a $1.1bn charge for OpenAI's warrants (up to ~10% of AMD at $0.01).
  • The stock fell ~15% Thursday with the group, then rose 11% Friday. (WSJ)

The biggest buyer is walking away from capacity that shows up late. Microsoft's Q3 capex ran ~$5bn below plan because third-party sites weren't ready, including Iren's $9.7bn Texas contract.

  • It declined a ~500MW expansion option with Nscale, and has declined to renew a 2023 deal to rent servers from Oracle.
  • Nscale is still preparing a $35bn IPO on $141mn of H1 revenue and a $1bn H1 loss.
  • Bearish: single-customer neoclouds and contract-backed debt; CoreWeave's 5-year CDS hit 1,262bp Thursday. (The Information, FT)

Nvidia now has a key-man problem on top of a policy problem. The FT reported Friday that US officials believe Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with a Megaspeed executive thought to be recruited by China's intelligence ministry. On Sunday he admitted an affair.

  • Nvidia's board opened an independent probe and says it has no reason to think secrets were compromised. House chairs want Huang at a Nov 18 briefing; Sen. Coons pledged a probe.
  • Megaspeed, a Singapore buyer of billions of dollars of Nvidia chips, was already under US investigation for possible smuggling to China.
  • Bearish: headline and export-control risk at a $4.5tn company run by a famously flat team (inference). (FT, WSJ)

Hot jobs keep a December hike on the table. October payrolls +165k vs ~59–65k expected; unemployment 4.1%; Aug–Sep revised up 84k; three-month average 137k.

  • Core PCE is 3.6% y/y. December hike odds sit near 78% (prediction markets), from 70% on Thursday (FedWatch).
  • The 2-year rose to 4.51% Friday, but yields still fell on the week. The Bank of England hiked to 4% (8–1). (FT)

Oil lost its war premium, then the weekend brought new Gulf strikes. Brent fell ~13% to ~$98 and WTI to ~$90 on bets a Democratic Congress pushes to end the Iran war; Qatar is mediating a Hormuz-for-blockade interim deal.

  • Sunday: Houthi missile shrapnel damaged Saudi Aramco's Riyadh offices; a tanker caught fire off Khasab, Oman; US forces disabled a ship trying to breach the Iran blockade.
  • Read: the price now assumes a deal that hasn't happened, so risk is skewed up from here (inference). (FT)

Washington and Beijing are both tightening control of the AI supply chain. The US told China to "immediately" deliver on the Busan truce (rare earths, farm purchases); the truce runs to Jan 10, and Trump and Xi meet at APEC in Shenzhen in about two weeks.

  • China blocked SoftBank's $5.4bn purchase of ABB's robotics unit; Oracle cancelled a $7bn Tencent AI deal under US pressure.
  • Japan is warning of a yttrium shortage as China restricts exports. (FT, WSJ)

The Through-Line

The AI trade spent October learning that capital is scarce; this week it learned that land and power are political. Demand still looks real (AMD's data-center sales doubled, Anthropic's run-rate passed $80bn, H100 rents near three-month highs), but the market cut what it will pay for future capacity, and crowded hedge-fund positioning turned that into a crash. Next comes the test of price: Anthropic's IPO, CoreWeave's results and a CPI print that decides whether the Fed adds a rate hike to the pile (inference).

Top Takeaways

  1. The Democratic sweep put data-center pause candidates into power in Texas, Georgia and Ohio, which hold over 40% of planned US capacity, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4% Thursday. Permits and grid hookups are local decisions, so the binding constraint on AI capex moved from chips and money to politics, and the market cut the value of every company selling into 2027 builds. (NVDA −9.30% Thu, +2.78% Fri)
  2. Memory and storage fell hardest Thursday (Sandisk −22%, Western Digital −17%) because their earnings depend most on new capacity, and they were among the most crowded hedge-fund longs. Forced de-grossing hits the most popular trades first, which is why Micron also bounced 5.1% Friday once selling eased. (MU −17.00% Thu, +5.12% Fri)
  3. October saw the biggest active unwind at stock-picking hedge funds in over a decade, and JPMorgan's clients had their worst day since April 2025 on Thursday. Funds that borrowed to own AI had to sell into falling prices, which turned a political shock into a $3.2tn rout; Walleye and others are cutting staff. (no single ticker; VIX 25.64 Fri)
  4. Anthropic plans to list the week of Nov 16, raising $80–100bn at $1.25–1.75tn, below the ~$2tn hoped for a month ago, after major investors said the price must fall. A cheaper IPO helps the deal clear, but it resets the mark for every lab, neocloud and supplier whose value leans on Anthropic's growth. (no public ticker)
  5. AI-linked companies have sold over $300bn of stock this year, with ~$100bn more queued, including Meta's proposed $60bn ATM. When equity markets turn, companies that fund capex by selling shares must sell more stock for the same cash or cut spending, as Iren just did. (CRWV +6.3% Fri, ~−50% in a month)
  6. AMD's data-center revenue nearly doubled to $8.5bn (64% of sales) and it guided Q4 to ~$17bn vs $16.23bn expected, because hyperscalers and OpenAI want a second GPU source to Nvidia. Today's AI demand is intact; the market is discounting how long it lasts. (AMD +11.04% Fri)
  7. Microsoft ran Q3 capex ~$5bn below plan because third-party capacity, including Iren's $9.7bn Texas contract, came late, and it declined to expand with Nscale or renew Oracle server rentals. A buyer with its own balance sheet can drop late suppliers, so execution risk lands on single-customer neoclouds and their lenders. (IREN capex cut; CRWV 5-year CDS 1,262bp Thu)
  8. Jensen Huang admitted an affair after the FT reported US officials believe his partner, a Megaspeed executive, was recruited by Chinese intelligence; Nvidia's board opened an independent probe. Nvidia's chip-export lobbying and Southeast Asian customers now face closer scrutiny from a Democratic Congress, adding key-man and policy risk to a $4.5tn stock. (NVDA +2.78% Fri)
  9. October payrolls rose 165k against ~60k expected and unemployment fell to 4.1%, with core PCE at 3.6%. Strong hiring gives the Fed no reason to rescue markets, so December hike odds stay near 78% and long-duration tech stays under pressure. (2-year 4.51%, +5bp Fri)
  10. Brent fell ~13% on the week to ~$98 on bets the new Congress pushes to end the Iran war, then Houthi strikes damaged Aramco's Riyadh offices on Sunday. Cheaper oil eased inflation fears midweek, but with no deal signed the risk premium can come back fast. (no single ticker; WTI ~$90)
