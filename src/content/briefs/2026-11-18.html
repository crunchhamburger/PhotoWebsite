Article Takeaways

Nvidia beat and raised, and the board shut the door on Jensen Huang. Fiscal third-quarter revenue was $115.8bn, 6.3% above the $108.9bn consensus and more than double a year earlier. Net income was $65.3bn.

  • Data-centre revenue rose 110% to $107.6bn on the Vera Rubin rollout. The current-quarter guide is $130bn ±3%, about 4% above the Street.
  • Interim CEO Colette Kress raised the growth forecast for the fiscal year to January 2028 to 75% from 70%.
  • Gross margin was 74.8% against 74% guided, but the guide for next quarter is 71–72% because the memory shortage is raising costs.
  • Supply commitments rose to $390bn from $279bn in one quarter, mostly multiyear memory contracts. Total commitments, with equity stakes, cloud and leases, are $518bn.
  • The catch: free cash flow was $43.8bn against roughly $57bn expected, and receivables rose almost a third to $81bn. The SEC is investigating whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors.
  • Chair Harvey Jones said the board unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and will not reinstate him, hours after Huang said he would return. The search covers insiders and outsiders. Huang and the general counsel brief House committees in early December.
  • China: Nvidia sold $2.4bn of older chips there last quarter and has now stopped.
  • The stock closed −0.44% on Tuesday, then rose 4.81% after hours to $162.94. It is down nearly 29% in a month.
  • Bullish: memory suppliers, and neoclouds near term (Nebius and CoreWeave rose after hours). Bearish: weaker Nvidia-financed borrowers, since Kress says she is looking harder at who gets help (inference).

(WSJ; FT; price notes)

Nvidia's own sales team pointed Alibaba to Megaspeed. Sales staff introduced Alibaba to Megaspeed founder Alice Huang twice in 2022, starting months before the August 2022 export controls, per three people with direct knowledge.

  • After the controls, Nvidia staff again suggested Megaspeed rent chips to Alibaba from Southeast Asia. Megaspeed wasn't the only firm introduced this way.
  • Alibaba as anchor customer helped the venture get loans from Asian regional banks to buy Nvidia servers.
  • Her network shipped billions of dollars of banned chips to China in 2023–2025. The Justice Department is leading a criminal probe.
  • The catch: remote access from Southeast Asia was legal until this week's Commerce rule. Nvidia says it cut ties with Megaspeed in June 2025.
  • Read-through: the legal exposure now reaches Nvidia's sales organisation, not only its former CEO (inference).

(The Information)

JPMorgan and Citi led a $2.8bn data-centre loan without looking hard at Megaspeed. Bain-controlled Bridge Data Centres raised the money in 2025 to expand in Thailand and Malaysia. Deutsche Bank, MUFG and SMBC were reportedly in the syndicate.

  • Lenders vetted the largest tenant, ByteDance. Megaspeed was also a tenant and drew little attention.
  • Red flags: Megaspeed's assets went from about $33m at end-2023 to nearly $3bn a year later.
  • Bridge replaced Megaspeed with another tenant before a new loan in May 2026.
  • The catch: no evidence the financed sites were used for diversion. But export rules bar knowingly financing unlicensed exports, and that includes wilful blindness.
  • Read-through: banks may pull back from Southeast Asian data-centre lending, raising funding costs there (inference).

(The Information)

Anthropic is set to price its IPO above the range tonight. Shares could price above $80, over the $70–$78 range, with trading on Nasdaq on Thursday.

  • At the original range it planned to sell about $61bn of shares, plus a $26bn private placement with investors including Google and Nvidia.
  • Demand: larger-than-usual hedge fund orders. Altimeter asked for $5bn. Bessemer, Menlo and Accel plan to buy more, not sell.
  • Sources differ on valuation: WSJ says advisers discussed $1.5tn–$1.6tn, the FT says $1.4tn or more. The original range implied about $1.33tn.
  • Amazon and Google will own close to 30% between them. At $1.4tn the FT puts Iconiq's stake at about $28bn and Lightspeed's at just over $25bn.
  • The catch: pricing depends on how the market takes Nvidia's results, and orders in hot IPOs are inflated.
  • Read-through: a strong debut marks up Amazon's and Alphabet's stakes. A $61bn-plus raise also competes for money with existing AI stocks (inference).

(WSJ; FT)

Microsoft is close to a $500m ByteDance deal for business Anthropic turned away. ByteDance is in advanced talks to spend about $500m more on Azure, mainly to use OpenAI models, per two people.

  • Part of that work used to go to Anthropic, which bars customers in prohibited countries. Microsoft is chasing similar deals with other cut-off Chinese firms, including Alibaba.
  • Microsoft can resell OpenAI models under a licence to 2032 and serves these customers from outside China.
  • OpenAI has complained that Chinese firms are distilling its models. Microsoft says it found no evidence of that at ByteDance.
  • Mustafa Suleyman told staff to prepare for a future in which OpenAI cuts off its IP.
  • The catch: a bipartisan House bill on distillation arrived in October, and political risk is high after the Nvidia scandal. China was under 1.5% of Microsoft's revenue in 2024.

(The Information)

The ECB compared the AI borrowing boom to subprime. Its Financial Stability Review puts Big Tech's off-balance-sheet commitments at more than $4tn as of the second quarter, well above what sits on their balance sheets.

  • How it works: tech groups sign contracts with data-centre developers and chip owners, who borrow against the promised cash flows. It acts like debt but the hyperscaler doesn't book it.
  • Circular deals add to it: cloud firms and AI labs borrow from chipmakers to buy their chips.
  • Even if AI works, the ECB sees risk of overcapacity and credit losses. It sees little sign so far of tech issuance crowding out other borrowers.
  • Also in the review: French long-term yields briefly topped 5% last month, and the spread over Bunds hit 1.75 points, the widest since 2011.
  • Bearish: neoclouds, data-centre developers and the private credit funds lending to them (inference).

(FT)

Wall Street expects profit growth to halve next year. Consensus has global earnings growth at 16% in 2027, down from 33.5% this year.

  • Goldman: US profit growth falls to 11% from 36%, and Asia ex-Japan to 26% from 74%.
  • Goldman expects AI capex growth to peak in the first half of 2027. Spending still rises, from $1.1tn in 2026 to $1.8tn in 2027.
  • Drags: slower hyperscaler capex growth hitting chip and memory makers, depreciation on AI assets, and higher interest costs.
  • Barclays has the S&P 500 at 8,700 at end-2027, about 9% above its end-2026 projection of 7,950.
  • The catch: strategists still expect index gains, but they rest on cheaper valuations, not earnings momentum.
