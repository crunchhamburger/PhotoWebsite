The week in one paragraph

Nvidia lost its founder and the bond market lost its nerve, yet stocks rose. Jensen Huang resigned after 33 years over two affairs, one with a suspected Chinese agent, and CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. Nvidia shed $573bn of value, the biggest weekly loss for any company on record, and closed below $4tn. The same week October CPI came in hot, the 10-year Treasury yield rose about 29bp to 5.15% (its worst week since April 2025), and a December Fed hike became fully priced. Stocks still snapped a four-week losing streak, helped by Monday's rebound and CoreWeave's results: Nasdaq 100 +3.5%, S&P 500 +2.5%, Nasdaq +2.3%, Dow +2.1%, Russell 2000 +1.3%. The market sold what depended on Nvidia's cheque book and bought what didn't. Next week tests all of it at once: Nvidia reports Tuesday under Kress, Anthropic prices Wednesday and trades Thursday, and Trump meets Xi in Shenzhen. Over the weekend Huang's allies began lobbying for his return while Treasury Secretary Bessent told the board to hire an outsider.

Article Takeaways

Jensen Huang is out, and Nvidia had the biggest weekly loss of market value any company has recorded. Huang resigned after reports of affairs with Megaspeed's Alice Huang, whom US officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence, and with an Nvidia executive. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and director Harvey Jones is the company's first chair.

  • Nvidia lost $573bn this week and closed at about $3.92tn, down more than $1.8tn from its Oct 6 peak of about $5.77tn. The stock ended at $162.85, −13% on the week, its sixth straight weekly fall, the longest run since 2012.
  • Succession is open. The board approached Arm's Rene Haas, says no formal search has started, and also has Kress and data-center head Ian Buck inside. AMD's Lisa Su is the other outsider named.
  • Weekend: Huang has discussed a comeback and allies are organizing a campaign, with a public letter backed by Elon Musk and Tim Sweeney. Shareholders Brad Gerstner and Gavin Baker asked the board to keep his return on the table. Bessent said the board should go outside and wants answers within 10 days.
  • Retail bought the dip: $1.96bn of Nvidia shares through Friday afternoon, the most since March 2025 (Vanda).
  • Bullish: AMD, Broadcom and anyone not reliant on Nvidia's financing. Bearish: Nvidia's governance discount until a CEO is named; Huang's ~$135bn stake is an overhang. (WSJ, FT, The Information)

The real exposure is Nvidia's cheque book, and the new boss is the one who tried to close it. Nvidia held $124bn of equity investments at end-July plus up to $105bn of credit support for OpenAI, $36bn of compute purchase commitments and $20bn of leases.

  • Kress pushed back on a proposed $250bn credit backstop for OpenAI covering 8GW; Nvidia split it and has guaranteed about $100bn so far. Nvidia recently raised its Anthropic investment to $10bn.
  • First casualty: Nscale told the SEC it needs more financing for 2027 obligations ahead of its $35bn IPO, after ordering chips for a $45bn Anthropic contract. It lost almost $1.7bn on $307mn of revenue in nine months.
  • One cloud customer told The Information it is now weighing Google and AMD chips, something it avoided before for fear of offending Huang.
  • Nvidia also lacks company-level D&O cover: it bought only $900mn of Side A insurance for directors and officers, so shareholder class actions fall on its own balance sheet. One suit was filed this week.
  • Bearish: Nvidia-financed neoclouds (CoreWeave −6.13% Friday, Iren −6.72%, Nebius −9.92%). Bullish: secured lenders and investment-grade customers (inference). (FT, The Information, WSJ)

The scandal reopened the China back door, and Washington is moving to shut it. Records seen by the WSJ show Megaspeed arranged nearly $12bn of top Nvidia chips for companies in China between March 2024 and June 2025.

  • Nvidia reported $12.4bn of China revenue in the six months to July 26, 7% of the total. Short-seller Culper Research estimates more than 20% of last year's revenue traced to China.
  • Commerce is drafting licensing rules that add Malaysia, Indonesia and Singapore and bar Chinese customers from remote access to Blackwell-class chips. Malaysia has drawn more than $34bn of data-center investment and says it is investigating Megaspeed.
  • Congress wants to attach export bills, including a Remote Access Security Act, to the must-pass NDAA in the lame-duck session. Three House chairs asked Huang, Nvidia's general counsel and a Commerce official to testify.
  • Bessent said Treasury backed blacklisting Megaspeed for months and blamed Commerce. The US and allies including Japan, the UK, South Korea, India and the UAE signed a declaration to protect AI supply chains ahead of the Trump–Xi meeting.
  • Bearish: Southeast Asian neoclouds serving ByteDance and Alibaba, and Nvidia's ex-US growth. Bullish: Chinese chip substitutes such as Cambricon (inference). (WSJ, FT, The Information)

Anthropic's IPO is oversubscribed and likely to price above its range. It filed to sell 825M shares at $70–78, raising more than $61bn at the midpoint, plus a $26bn private placement from investors including Google, Nvidia and Microsoft. Pricing is Nov 18; trading as ANT starts Nov 19.

  • The range values it at $1.26–1.41tn (WSJ: about $1.33tn). Bankers may lift it to $1.5tn or more, which The Information puts at $84–85 a share. Above $75bn raised it passes SpaceX's June record.
  • Dario Amodei told Jamie Dimon he hopes the deal will "underpromise and overdeliver" and called compute spending the company's deepest dilemma.
  • Up to 20% of shares may go to individuals, but retail is cooler than at SpaceX's listing: net retail turnover fell about 40% from May to September (Vanda).
  • Valuation math: about $100bn run-rate by year-end, doubling in two years at a 25% margin, gives roughly $50bn of 2028 operating profit, so the range is over 25x 2028 EBIT. Morningstar's fair value is $1.7tn.
  • Funds must sell something to buy it; AI hardware is the obvious source, which may explain part of the chip weakness (inference). (WSJ, FT, The Information)

Hot inflation gave Treasuries their worst week since April 2025. October CPI rose 0.4% m/m against 0.3% expected and core rose 0.3% against 0.2%. PPI rose 0.3% m/m but 6.5% y/y, the fastest since November 2022.

  • The 10-year ended at 5.15%, up about 29bp, after touching 5.17%. The 2-year rose more than 30bp to about 4.88%, its biggest weekly rise since late 2024.
  • A 25bp December hike to 4–4.25% is fully priced, with about a 10% chance of 50bp. A second hike in January is largely priced, against 10% odds a week ago, and a third by June.
  • Waller floated 50bp; Hammack, Goolsbee, Daly and Collins all leaned hawkish. Funds that had bought bonds after September's sell-off were forced out.
  • The Fed's stability report put hedge-fund leverage at its highest since data began in 2013 and named AI valuations as a top risk.
  • Bearish: long-duration tech and debt-funded AI capacity. Bonds stopped hedging tech this week. (FT, WSJ)

CoreWeave showed the strong names can still raise money, just not in the unsecured bond market. Q3 revenue was $3.7bn (+168% y/y), backlog reached $132.5bn and the full-year guide rose to $13–13.3bn.

  • It plans to fund up to $39bn of 2026 capex with chip-backed loans at about 7–7.5% and about $9bn of customer prepayments. Its unsecured 2032 bond yields about 15%.
  • Q3 capex was $14.5bn against $2bn of operating cash flow; debt plus leases total $66.2bn.
  • The stock rose 24.71% Wednesday, then gave much of it back as Nvidia's crisis hit neoclouds: about −7% Thursday and −6.13% Friday.
  • Bullish: secured, customer-backed financing. Bearish: any neocloud that needs unsecured debt or a Nvidia guarantee. (WSJ, FT)

OpenAI is growing but burning, and its round got smaller and cheaper. It closed $11B at a $1.25T pre-money valuation toward a $20B round; the original goal was $30B at $1.4T.

  • Q3 revenue rose about 50% to roughly $10B, with a $10.9B adjusted operating loss.
  • Anthropic's Q3: $17B revenue and a $567M operating loss, after a $974M profit in Q2, because of pricier short-term compute. It added about $10B of Azure and Google Cloud commitments and said Lambda's $34.9B lease can be cancelled.
  • OpenAI's $1.25T mark set the floor of Anthropic's range, investors told The Information.
  • Read: the lab with the better unit economics is the one going public; the other is leaning on partners (inference). (WSJ, The Information)

SoftBank put the first hard number on cancellation costs in AI power. It lost $7bn in the quarter, including a $3.7bn write-off on nonrefundable deposits for more than 13GW of gas turbines, mostly GE Vernova's.

  • Its shares are down more than 55% since June and its loan-to-value is an estimated 18–22% against a 25% ceiling.
  • Freed turbine slots ease a bottleneck for other builders (inference). (FT, WSJ, The Information)

The "picks and shovels" trade has lost $4.2tn since June. An FT analysis of manufacturers, utilities and miners tied to data-center construction found they gave back almost half of the $9.4tn gained in the prior 12 months.

  • Caterpillar is down 44% from its June 30 high, a $214bn loss. Oklo is down 49%; Comfort Systems and Corning have roughly halved.
  • Industrials are −9% since June 30, the second-worst S&P sector after technology (−11%). Energy is +22%.
  • Orders say otherwise: Caterpillar's backlog is a record $78bn, Siemens' data-center backlog grew 60% to €7bn, and US data-center construction spending hit $96bn in the year to September. One caution: buyers double-order when supply is scarce.
  • Amazon revived a 600MW deal with Fermi in Texas, with a $200mn prepayment; permitted sites gain value as Texas moves to restrict new ones. Fermi has $286.3mn of cash against about $958mn of debt.
  • Bullish: already-permitted power. Bearish: industrials priced like chip stocks. (FT)

Investors rotated rather than left. Since Oct 6 the S&P's tech sector is −14.3%, while healthcare, staples and utilities turned up. The equal-weight S&P 500 is +3.9% over that span; the cap-weighted index is −2.9%.

  • Walmart is up about 20% since Oct 6, Coca-Cola 13% and Johnson & Johnson 12%. Global staples had their best month since 1998 in October.
  • Valuations have converged: tech at 19x forward earnings, utilities 17.3x, healthcare and staples above 20x.
  • The S&P 500 is only about 4% below its October peak. Defensives are no longer cheap, so the rotation has less room (inference). (FT)

Oil swung to a surplus, but the Gulf is heating up again. The IEA says supply beat demand by almost 2mn b/d in October, the first surplus since the US–Iran war began. Brent fell below $90 on Tuesday for the first time since August, then ended the week at $95.84.

  • Iran attacked Bahrain on Friday and Sunday, the first strikes there in two months, and says two tankers hit mines in Hormuz. Trump said the US doesn't need a deal with Iran.
  • Diamondback is close to buying EnCap-backed Double Eagle V for about $5bn in cash and stock, extending Permian consolidation.
  • Cheaper crude helps inflation later, but CPI shows price pressure is broader than energy. (FT)

The Through-Line

The AI trade lost three kinds of support at once: Nvidia's balance sheet (the person who questioned the guarantees now runs the company), cheap money (the 10-year is back above 5% with three hikes priced by June) and export latitude (Congress, Commerce and Treasury all want the Southeast Asia route closed). Demand did not break: CoreWeave's backlog grew, H100 rents ended higher than a week ago and Anthropic's book is several times covered. So the market is sorting by who funds themselves. Self-financed and secured names held; those leaning on Nvidia guarantees, unsecured debt or share sales fell (inference).
