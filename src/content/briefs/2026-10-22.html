Thursday, Oct 22, 2026 · FT, WSJ, The Information, prices

Article Takeaways

Meta said capital for AI is getting scarce, and the AI trade broke. Meta's filing said it will raise money early because Alphabet's and SpaceX's big raises, plus expected Anthropic and OpenAI fundraising, are making capital scarcer. Its future spending commitments are now ~$825B, up from ~$700B in July.

  • Thursday: Nasdaq Composite −2.6% (worst day since June), Nasdaq 100 −2.9%, S&P 500 −1.2%, Dow flat. The Philly semis index fell ~6.7%. Meta −7% (~$124B of market value), SpaceX −9.1%.
  • Bonds rallied: the 10-year fell to just under 5%, its biggest one-day drop since at least 2024. Meta 5-year CDS widened ~10bp to ~116bp, the highest since those contracts started trading.
  • SK Hynix dropped the US IPO of Solidigm, its California memory unit, citing market conditions rather than the unit's performance. CoreWeave 2032 bonds sit just under 80c, down from ~90c last month.
  • The catch: Bessent privately told investors Wednesday he sees no systemic problem in the AI boom. An AllianceBernstein PM says financing limits are now a real growth headwind into 2027 for externally funded builders.
  • Bearish: semis, power and cooling suppliers, neoclouds, debt-funded builders. Bullish: Treasurys, staples, regional banks. (WSJ, FT)

Meta beat on revenue, then built a new way to sell stock. Q3 revenue was $64.6B (+26%, a record). Net income was $9.8B vs $17.2B expected, after a $9.9B legal charge.

  • A new nonvoting share class is paid as a dividend on Jan. 19, 2027, effectively a 2-for-1 split. Zuckerberg keeps voting control and may sell 20% of his new nonvoting shares right away.
  • Q4 guide $73–76B. The 2026 capex floor rose to $135B (range $135–145B). FCF was −$2.1B (vs −$9.4B expected), its first negative FCF as a public company. No 2027 capex number.
  • Muse, its paid AI agent ($20 and $100/month), passed 10M downloads. Truist sees $28.5B of extra revenue by 2030.
  • The catch: legal overhang of an $18B 48-state settlement and a $25B New Mexico judgment ordered Wednesday.
  • Read-through: nonvoting stock gives Meta an equity funding channel without touching control, and adds equity supply just as AI IPOs line up (inference). (WSJ)

OpenAI marked its round down to $1.25T. It aims to close a $30B round by Oct. 30 at up to $1.25T, below the $1.4T pitched earlier this month but ~50% above its current $852B mark.

  • Coatue and UAE funds are set to lead. The deal also gives OpenAI right of first refusal on new compute built by its UAE partners.
  • Morgan Stanley and Dragoneer pitched it to wealth clients at $1.4T, and demand was weak; one source called it "a bit of a thud." It may raise less than $30B.
  • The round is a bridge to an IPO, likely in 1H 2027. Some investors want to see how Anthropic's IPO trades first.
  • Read-through: the first visible down-pricing in private AI marks (inference). (The Information, FT)

The AI-infrastructure IPO window is shutting. SB Energy pushed its IPO from September to December or 2027 and is waiting to see how Anthropic's IPO, expected before Thanksgiving, trades.

  • It wanted $5–7B for the Ohio campus OpenAI will lease. The site targets 8.5GW and ~$600B of Nvidia hardware, needs >$100B in total, and has a $105B Nvidia credit guarantee.
  • The catch: bankers floated ~$60B, but H1 revenue was $139M, a >200x annualized multiple. Its $430B of contracted revenue starts phasing in from 2028.
  • Peers: Firmus pulled its IPO, DayOne may delay or shrink, and AirTrunk postponed a ~$1.5B Singapore REIT. Nscale still targets a fall listing at ~$30B.
  • Read-through: funding shifts to SoftBank, Nvidia and private money; Anthropic's debut decides the ECM pipeline (inference). (The Information)

Nvidia's banned B300 chips showed up on a China Telecom network. Security firm Lava found 32 Blackwell Ultra B300s tied to China Telecom through exposed Nvidia monitoring endpoints. The B300 is barred from sale to China.

  • The catch: IP addresses don't prove where the chips physically are, and Lava didn't show how they got there. Nvidia says smuggling is not a real factor.
  • Nvidia announced optional location-checking software in December but hasn't shipped it.
  • Bearish: export-control headline risk for NVDA, and possible pressure for mandatory chip-location checks (inference). (The Information)

Tesla cut capex and its car ambitions while it studies a SpaceX merger. The new target is 5M vehicles a year by 2030, down from 20M. Tesla delivered 1.6M last year.

  • Q3 revenue was flat at $28B, adjusted net income $1.7B (−4%), and GAAP net income $787M (−44%). Auto gross margin ex-credits was 18%.
  • Capex was $4.7B vs $7.9B expected. Full-year guidance fell to ~$20B from $25B, and FCF was >$1.5B vs an expected burn of >$4B.
  • The catch: Musk says AI spending must rise sharply, yet capex is running well below plan. Special committees are studying a SpaceX merger, and there's a joint Terafab chip plant in Austin.
  • Read-through: better near-term cash, but the equity story now rests on AI, robotics and related-party deals (inference). (FT)

Blackstone beat on earnings while its exits dried up. Distributable earnings were $1.7B ($1.32/share vs $1.52 a year ago), above estimates. Fee-related earnings rose 6% to $1.57B, slightly short.

  • Net realizations fell 34%: PE −49%, real estate −21%. Credit and insurance rose 43%, helped by AI-infrastructure borrowing.
  • The Fed's mid-September hike slowed exits and IPOs and ended hopes of a 2026 deal rebound. Schwarzman expects 2027 to be strong.
  • Read-through: private credit wins from AI debt demand, while PE exits and M&A and ECM fees stay weak while rates are high (inference). (WSJ)

Optimum (ex-Altice USA) is preparing Chapter 11 to hand control to creditors. The plan restructures ~$26B of debt, possibly before year-end; ~$6B matures in 2027.

  • Apollo, Oaktree and JPMorgan are expected to get majority control. It ends a two-way lawsuit fight between Optimum and its creditors.
  • The catch: a debt-for-equity swap could trigger a >$4B tax bill if subsidiaries get separated.
  • Underlying problem: broadband subscribers leaving for fiber and wireless. A win for creditor co-ops and restructuring advisers (inference). (WSJ)

Oil hit $113 as Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalated. Brent settled +4% at $113.17, near early Iran-war levels, and WTI rose 2.4% to $99.47. The US ordered some staff out of Saudi Arabia.

  • France's 10-year rose to 5.08%. The OAT–Bund spread is >1.7pp, the widest since 2011, and Belgium's spread is the highest since 2012. (FT)

India has spent ~$90B defending the rupee since June. The rupee is −5% this year to ~Rs94.5/$, hit by oil and record foreign equity selling. Reserves are ~$60B below September's $785B peak.

  • The RBI hiked 25bp to 5.5%, and ANZ calls the pace of spending unsustainable. Jio Platforms IPO inflows are giving the rupee temporary support. (FT)

Turkey cut rates to 36% despite an $18B investment-fund scandal. It is the first cut since the Iran war began. Inflation is 29.7%, a near five-year low, but consumer confidence fell 7.7%.

  • Weak demand is doing the disinflation work. The consensus expects a 100bp cut in December. (FT)

Russia and Ukraine traded record-scale strikes on the day peace talks resumed. Russia hit Kyiv with 20+ missiles, some with cluster warheads, plus 210 drones. Ukraine sent 600+ drones toward Moscow.

  • Witkoff met Putin, and trilateral talks may restart in November. There's no energy ceasefire, so strikes on refineries keep a risk premium in oil (inference). (FT)

The Through-Line

The constraint on AI just moved from chips and power to money. Meta says capital is getting scarce, OpenAI cut its asking price, SB Energy and Solidigm pulled IPOs, and Meta's CDS hit a record. Everything now waits on how Anthropic's IPO trades. Lower Treasury yields help on paper, but oil at $113 and 5% French yields keep the cost of capital high (inference).

  1. Meta's filing said it will need to raise hundreds of billions of dollars for AI, and will start early, because rivals' raises are making capital scarce, and its commitments jumped to ~$825B. The largest self-funded AI spender is now saying cash flow isn't enough, so every AI builder's financing gets repriced: chips, cooling and neoclouds sold off together. (META −6.98%)
  2. The Philly semis index fell ~6.7% and the Nasdaq had its worst day since June. Investors moved from AI capex to bonds and staples because the AI build-out depends on external capital that is getting more expensive and less available. Lam Research, Coherent and Lumentum each fell ~12%. (NVDA Oct 22 move not found; chipmakers −~7%)
  3. Meta created a nonvoting share class, effective Jan. 19, 2027, and turned FCF-negative (−$2.1B) after a $9.9B legal charge. Nonvoting stock lets Meta sell equity to fund capex without diluting Zuckerberg's control, which adds equity supply just before Anthropic and OpenAI list (inference). (META −6.98%)
  4. OpenAI cut its round target to $1.25T from the $1.4T pitch after weak wealth-channel demand, aiming for $30B by Oct. 30. It's the first visible markdown in private AI. Investors are waiting on Anthropic's IPO price, so a weak debut would hit OpenAI's 1H 2027 listing and the AI-infrastructure debt trade (inference). (no public ticker)
  5. SB Energy delayed its IPO to December or 2027, and SK Hynix pulled Solidigm's US listing.   7. Tesla cut 2026 capex guidance to ~$20B from $25B and its 2030 target to 5M cars from 20M, while special committees study a SpaceX merger. Lower capex lifts near-term FCF (>$1.5B), but the stock now trades on AI, robotics and a related-party deal Musk sits on both sides of. (TSLA −3.08%)

An oil shock keeps inflation, and so Fed-hike risk, alive even as AI fears pull yields down, and it is draining oil importers like India (~$90B spent on the rupee). (no single ticker)
