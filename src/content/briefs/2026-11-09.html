Article Takeaways

Nvidia's board is investigating whether Jensen Huang was a Chinese spy target. Independent directors and outside counsel will review reports that Huang had an affair with Alice Huang, a former top executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore Nvidia customer. US officials concluded last year that she was passing information to Beijing.

  • The board says it sees no sign that secrets or national security were compromised, and it backs Huang as CEO. Huang apologized to staff on Sunday.
  • Commerce and the DOJ have probed Megaspeed for over a year over possible chip diversion to China. Whether to add it and dozens of others to the Entity List now sits with US–China trade negotiators.
  • Sen. Cornyn wants a congressional probe and three House Republicans want a briefing. Trump says Huang should stay, and Beijing called the story slander.
  • Bearish: Nvidia's key-person and China-export risk premium. Huang is the main voice for looser chip exports, so any finding helps the hawks. AMD and Broadcom rose on the day. (WSJ)

FT: the scandal puts a price on how much Nvidia depends on one man. FT had Nvidia down 7.5% in early trading, more than $300bn of value, on a market cap of about $4.5tn.

  • US officials believe the Ministry of State Security recruited the Megaspeed executive. A government memo on it was drafted just before Trump's May summit with Xi.
  • Nvidia runs flat: about six core executives and dozens of direct reports to Huang. That makes key-person risk unusually real.
  • House China committee chair John Moolenaar wants Huang to testify. Huang's H200 export push had already done little for sales because Beijing pushed back.
  • Sources differ on the Monday move: WSJ's page showed −5.76%, The Information −6.6%, FT −7.5% early and −6% later. (FT)

Last week showed how fast the AI trade can break when systematic money sells. Thursday's drop, led by Nvidia and Micron, put the Nasdaq into a correction and erased about $2.5T of value, the worst index day since April 2025.

  • Barclays estimates vol-control funds cut equity exposure by 30 percentage points, the most in one day since Feb 2020. Options volume hit a record.
  • The trigger was the Democratic sweep, including a Texas governor who campaigned on a data-center moratorium and new governors in Ohio and Georgia.
  • Over the week the S&P 500 fell about 2.5%, tech about 7% and energy about 12%.
  • Man Group expects projects to take longer and cost more. Wellington sees a slower build-out than priced, and Key Private Bank sees buying chances. (WSJ)

AI companies have sold over $300B of stock this year, and investors are tiring of it. About $100B more is queued, mostly through at-the-market programs, including a proposed $60B Meta ATM for early 2027. All US IPOs and follow-ons in 2025 came to $194B.

  • Alphabet's first equity sale in about 20 years includes a $40B ATM, with $12.4B sold by Sept 30.
  • CoreWeave has an ATM for up to 35M shares but has disclosed no sales; it reports Tuesday. Iren has raised $3.7B of a $6B ATM, and its stock is down about 48% since March.
  • Anthropic plans to start IPO marketing this week and list the week of Nov 16.
  • The catch: lower share prices mean more dilution for the same cash, and buyers want better terms. Some issuers may switch back to debt. (The Information)

OpenAI cut its fundraise in both size and price. It closed an $11B first tranche at a $1.25T pre-money valuation, toward a $20B round. The original goal was $30B at $1.4T.

  • An investor call on Q3 results is expected next week.
  • Bearish read for Anthropic's IPO price and for OpenAI-linked suppliers' backlog confidence (inference). (The Information)

SoftBank is the levered bet on AI sentiment, and the sell-off is testing it. Its shares and Arm's are both down about 60% since June. It reports half-year results Tuesday.

  • Loan-to-value is estimated at 18–22%, up from 13% in June, against a 25% target. That leaves $10–26bn of borrowing room.
  • About $65bn is committed to OpenAI. The SB Energy IPO at a $50bn valuation could be shelved, and its biggest sites are in Texas.
  • China blocked its $5.4bn ABB robotics deal. CDS spreads widened sharply. (FT)

TSMC is weighing a move into 3D DRAM at Nvidia's request. The memory shortage is severe enough that Apple raised device prices this year. Options include new fabs or converting an existing plant; Micron paid $1.8B for a Powerchip fab in March.

  • There is no timeline or scale yet, and TSMC says it is focused on logic. It last reported memory revenue in Q2 2008.
  • The catch: 3D DRAM yields are hard and could take years, and memory supply often lands after demand fades. It also sits awkwardly with TSMC's HBM partnership with SK Hynix.
  • Bearish long-term for Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron pricing power. Positive for Nvidia's leverage over suppliers. Little effect on 2027 supply (inference). (The Information)

Stocks bounced Monday, but memory lagged. Early in New York the S&P 500 was +1.4% and the Nasdaq 100 +1.8%. TSMC rose 4.7% and the Taiex 4.9% after Taiwan curbed short selling; Samsung rose only 0.6% and SK Hynix 0.4%.

  • Brent fell about 5% to $93.58 and WTI to just under $87 after Trump called fresh strikes on Iran highly unlikely.
  • JPMorgan AM called the sell-off an overreaction. BNP sees a broad rotation out of Asian AI-supply-chain names. (FT)

Big banks are less exposed to Washington than to the AI slowdown. Since the election the KBW Bank index is down 1.7%, against about −2.5% for the S&P 500 and more than −11% for chip stocks.

  • The Fed stays in Trump-appointee hands (Bowman to 2029, Warsh to 2030), so capital relief continues. The main bill risk is a card APR cap, which TD Cowen expects nearer 36% than 10%.
  • Banks trade at about 1.5x book, 0.5x above their 15-year average; JPMorgan is above 2.5x.
  • The real risk is that AI deal flow stalls and hits IB and trading fees. Goldman and Morgan Stanley have fallen more than big banks since Tuesday. (WSJ)

China's factory-gate prices hit a four-year high on the oil shock. October PPI rose 5.7% y/y (5.3% expected), while CPI slowed to 0.7%.

  • Oil, coal and fuel processing prices rose 35%. Weak demand means firms can't pass costs on, so margins get squeezed. (FT)

The Through-Line

The sell-off is moving from share prices into funding. OpenAI shrank its round, SoftBank's leverage is rising, about $100B of AI stock supply is still queued, and Anthropic's IPO next week is the test of who will pay. On top of that, the market's largest company now carries key-person and China-export risk (inference).
