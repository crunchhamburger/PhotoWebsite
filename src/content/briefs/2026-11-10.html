Article Takeaways

Anthropic cut its IPO range before launch and still aims for about $1.33T. It is filing to sell 825M shares, including insider sales, at $70–$78. That raises more than $61B at the midpoint, and trading is expected Thursday of next week.

  • A week ago investors expected about $1.5T, and about $2T was floated months ago. Some big investors pushed the price down after Thursday's AI sell-off.
  • A concurrent private placement of up to $26B, with Google and AMD among the buyers, could take the total past $80B. That is about the $75B SpaceX raised.
  • Run-rate revenue was about $81B at the end of October, per the filing. At $1.33T that is roughly 16x run-rate revenue (inference).
  • The catch: bankers sometimes set a low range so they can raise it later. The deal is being read as a test of the whole AI trade. (WSJ)

FT: Anthropic's chip and cloud suppliers are also its anchor buyers. The $26bn placement at the IPO price comes from Nvidia, AMD, Micron, Broadcom, TSMC, Google and Microsoft.

  • Fully diluted valuation is $1.26–1.41tn, up from $965bn in May's private round. Run-rate revenue rose $10bn in October alone.
  • With a $9.7bn greenshoe and the placement, the total raise could reach about $100bn at the top of the range. SpaceX's June IPO ($1.78tn, $86bn raised) is still the record.
  • Each of the seven co-founders sells about $250mn and keeps about $18bn.
  • Backers are counting on index funds and large managers as "forced buyers" while tech sells off.
  • Sources differ on size: WSJ says over $80B total and ~$1.33T; FT says up to ~$100bn and $1.26–1.41tn. The gap is the greenshoe and the top-of-range basis. (FT)

October CPI came in hot, and a December hike now looks locked in. Headline CPI rose 0.4% m/m against 0.3% expected (September was 0.6%). Core rose 0.3% against 0.2% expected.

  • There is no 12-month rate: October 2025 prices were never collected because of last fall's shutdown.
  • CME futures price about 90% odds of a 25bp December hike and about 10% for 50bp. Waller has said a third warm month could put 50bp on the table.
  • Strength is broad, across core goods and services, not just energy. Airfares were up 22% y/y through September.
  • Bearish for long-duration growth and AI multiples. The 10-year was about 4.97% premarket, up 7bp. (WSJ)

FT: the 2-year yield jumped as much as 20bp to 4.74%. The Fed already hiked in September to 3.75–4%, its first rise in more than three years.

  • FedWatch: 25bp at 89% (up from 84%), 50bp at 11%, no change at 0%. CNBC put 50bp odds at 13.6%, so sources differ slightly.
  • Drivers include petrol, electricity, food, car insurance and medical care. Background: the Iran war, tariffs and the AI construction boom.
  • The S&P 500 opened down 0.6% and the Nasdaq 100 down 0.7%. (FT)

CoreWeave's bonds now price its new debt near 15%. Its most-traded junk bond (9.625% due 2032) yielded about 15% on Monday, and the shares are down about 40% in a month. It reports after Tuesday's close; Nebius reports Thursday.

  • If 15% is the going rate for non-hyperscaler debt, some chip and data-center plans stop working, and higher compute prices could slow AI adoption.
  • The offset: wide spreads on old bonds don't change current funding costs, and secured debt backed by data centers or hyperscaler contracts (e.g. Meta) has held up much better.
  • Bullish for secured lenders and private credit. Bearish for unsecured neocloud bondholders (inference). (WSJ)

The Information: Huang's ties to Alice Huang helped move at least $7.5B of Nvidia chips through Chinese firms. Two people who know her say the relationship goes back to at least 2023.

  • Shanghai Century Litong was founded in June 2023 and named a preferred Nvidia cloud partner by October 2023, two weeks after the US banned A800/H800 sales.
  • About 240,000 banned chips, mostly H100/H200, reached China; some served Alibaba and Tencent. Megaspeed, where she was MD in 2023, imported about $4.6B of Nvidia hardware in 2023–25 (Bloomberg).
  • Nvidia says its partners must follow export rules and its own checks have led to prosecutions. Century Litong denies dealings with her.
  • Huang faces a closed-door congressional briefing next week. US and Singapore probes have largely stopped the trade. (The Information)

Trump says the FBI is looking into Huang, and Nvidia's own story is under scrutiny. Huang told Trump he didn't know she was a spy, and Trump says he believes him. Nvidia's board has opened its own investigation.

  • Nvidia's general counsel told staff as recently as last week that the affair claims were false. Lawmakers will ask who knew.
  • Trump meets Xi in Shenzhen next week and says he won't raise it.
  • WSJ's long read (text didn't load) flags Huang's lobbying for looser export controls and Nvidia's lack of a succession plan.
  • Bearish: key-person risk at a ~$4.3tn company, and China licence relief now looks less likely (inference). (FT; WSJ)

The Information: export-control bills are moving again, and the White House may not block them. Three bills sit in the Senate NDAA, which is expected to pass before January.

  • Match Act: chipmaking tools including lithography, with pressure on the Netherlands and Japan. Overwatch Act: oversight of chip licences to China. Chip Security Act: chip tracking.
  • The White House asked for a Commerce rule banning remote access to restricted chips (the Singapore/Malaysia route) and to limit Match to advanced production.
  • The catch: Commerce won't act without Trump's go-ahead, and last year's GAIN AI Act died after White House pushback.
  • Bearish for Nvidia's China and Southeast Asia demand and for ASML's China service revenue (inference). (The Information)

SoftBank lost $7bn as it wrote off $3.7bn of data-center equipment deposits. The fiscal Q2 net loss was ¥1.1tn against an expected ¥84bn profit, its first quarterly loss in almost two years.

  • The write-off covers non-refundable deposits on gas turbines, grid gear and turbine slots, including the Ports-Pike, Ohio site leased to OpenAI. Total deposits to date are $9.5B; $0.9B of the loss is contract changes and settlements.
  • Executives said their view of what AI data centers need has changed. The CFO says there are no talks to downsize Ohio and up to $2.1bn of rebates are possible.
  • Funding: a record $56.5bn raised in the quarter, loan-to-value 15.9%, and a ¥270.1bn loss on the Intel stake. About $65bn is committed to OpenAI.
  • Bearish for turbine and grid-equipment order visibility. The $50bn SB Energy IPO may slip. (The Information; FT)

Oil fell below $90 as Trump stepped back from new Iran strikes. Brent dropped as much as 3.7% to $89.16, its first time under $90 since August. WTI fell about 2.4% to $84.74.

  • Brent is down about 24% in November, on track for its biggest monthly fall since 2020.
  • The catch: there is no deal, and the Strait of Hormuz has been effectively shut since late February.
  • Helps airlines, consumers and energy importers. Lower oil could eventually ease central-bank pressure (inference). (FT)

Investors are hiding in defensives on fears the AI bull market is over. Global consumer staples rose 10.6% in October, their best month since 1998, and healthcare rose 9.8%. MSCI World Tech is down more than 13% since early October.

  • Walmart is up over 15% since Oct 6; P&G and Coca-Cola over 10%.
  • Citi cut US and global tech to neutral and upgraded Europe ex-UK, citing wider credit spreads and collapsing AI sentiment.
  • The catch: defensives are now expensive, and crowded trades snap back fast. (FT)

The Through-Line

The AI story today is about the cost of money, not demand. A hot CPI pushed up rates, CoreWeave's bonds price new debt near 15%, SoftBank is eating cancellation costs on power gear, and Anthropic trimmed its range and lined up its own suppliers as buyers. Meanwhile H100 rental prices rose again, so compute is still scarce; funding it is what got harder (inference).
