Week ending Fri Oct 16, 2026 · Sources: WSJ, Financial Times, The Information, CNBC/CBS/TheStreet/Yahoo Finance

The week in one paragraph. Oil (the Iran war plus Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia; Brent ~$110) pushed September CPI to 3.7% and the 10-year Treasury yield above 5.3%, its highest since 2002. The AI trade then cracked at its weakest point: the debt that funds data centers. Failed data-center IPOs (Firmus, AirTrunk), distressed CoreWeave bonds and Anthropic paying ~10% on a $60bn chip financing triggered a risk-off week. The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.5%, its worst week since the April 2025 tariff shock. Nvidia lost more than $350bn in market value and chip stocks (SOX) fell more than 11%. Treasuries rallied as a safe haven even on hot inflation (10-year ~5.08%), and money rotated into energy, financials, defensives and the Dow (+0.7%).

1. Macro: inflation, rates, the Fed

  • September CPI (released Oct 14): headline +0.6% m/m and 3.7% y/y (from 3.4%). Core +0.3% m/m and 2.6% y/y. Both were slightly above consensus.
  • What drove it: energy. Gasoline rose 9.1% in the month, about half the headline gain. Fuel oil is up ~74% y/y and airfares ~22% y/y. US diesel averages ~$6.31/gal, near the $6.53 record, and the IEA sees a global diesel shortage until mid-2027.
  • Fed (FOMC Oct 27–28): after a September hike to 3.75–4.00%, October hike odds spiked from ~20% to ~35–48% on CPI day. The bond rally and Chair Warsh giving no forward guidance moved pricing back toward a hold. Check CME FedWatch before relying on a number.
  • Bonds as a safe haven: the 10-year fell as much as 15bp midweek despite the hot inflation data. Investors were fleeing the AI/credit scare, and crowded short positions in bonds were being unwound.
  • Global rates: the ECB (euro-area inflation 3.8%) and BoE (UK CPI ~3.4% expected) both face rate-hike risk. The 10-year German Bund is ~3.41%.
  • Energy politics: ahead of the midterms, Trump waived new Congress-mandated Russia sanctions, earlier lifted sanctions on Russian diesel, and is weighing letting sanctioned "shadow fleet" tankers be sold to legitimate operators to cut freight and diesel costs. Analysts expect only a marginal effect because Middle East war risk is the real driver.

The link to tech: high oil keeps headline inflation up, which keeps the Fed tightening and long-term yields high. That squeezes debt-funded AI infrastructure companies and richly valued tech stocks first.

2. AI and tech: the financing layer is cracking

The IPO window is closing on AI infrastructure

  • Firmus (Australia; backed by Nvidia and Blackstone) pulled its IPO at a ~A$44bn (~$30bn) valuation. Investors balked at the valuation, debt of ~6× projected 2028 earnings, and lock-ups. Underwriters included Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan and BofA. (FT, The Information)
  • AirTrunk (Blackstone) cancelled a ~$1.5bn Singapore REIT listing. SB Energy delayed its IPO with $174bn of capital needs against $430bn of contracted revenue. The FT reports OpenAI also delayed its IPO.
  • Nscale is still pitching a $35bn New York IPO, but Microsoft cancelled a lease on its 50MW UK site because grid power is unlikely before 2030. Anthropic can walk away from a $45bn Nscale deal if delivery slips. (FT)
  • DayOne (Singapore, spun out of China's GDS) is pressing ahead with a New York IPO of up to $5bn even though ByteDance supplied ~69% of its first-half revenue. Oracle is No. 2 at 15%. (The Information, exclusive)

Neocloud debt stress

  • CoreWeave: the stock fell ~25% on the week, bonds fell below 80 cents (~15% yield), and its 5-year CDS went above 11 points. Its CEO said the failed peer deals were weighing on how investors see CoreWeave. Jensen Huang's public vote of confidence sparked a brief +6% bounce Thursday.
  • Nebius also fell ~24% over five days. The FT says investors are dumping data-center bonds.
  • Contrast with Dell (WSJ, Christopher Mims): Dell builds AI servers only to order and has a negative cash conversion cycle, so in effect customers finance its inventory. It has $95bn in AI server back-orders, but the stock still fell more than 10% after the interview. Because Dell builds to order, any pullback in demand would show up in its revenue almost immediately, which makes it an early warning sign.

AI labs' cost of capital and revenue

  • Anthropic "Project North Star": a $60bn package to fund ~1.5GW of Google TPUs. It has ~$42bn of Broadcom-guaranteed senior debt and $18bn of junior loans, of which Blackstone takes $9bn at a ~10% coupon versus ~7% for comparable BB credit. Anthropic's June deal paid 8.5%. KBRA rates Anthropic one notch below investment grade, with an upgrade expected after its IPO. (FT)
  • Anthropic Q3 revenue rose ~50% to $16.8bn, which markets read as slower than hoped and was one trigger of the midweek chip rout.
  • OpenAI ads reached ~$1bn annualized (versus ~$50bn annualized for the whole company). Advertisers tripled to ~60k after big price cuts (~$5 a click versus $30–50 on Google for some keywords) and hires from Meta. It is still likely to miss the $2.4bn 2026 ad target it gave investors. (The Information)
  • OpenAI is in talks to buy Trail of Bits, a security-research firm, and hired a former Wiz executive as CRO. The AI labs are moving into cybersecurity, a long-term threat to Palo Alto and CrowdStrike. (The Information)
  • Commoditization: the top-10 AI models' share of developer usage fell from 55% to 14% in a year as cheaper "good enough" models win. That pressures frontier labs' pricing and IPO valuations. (The Information Pro, partly paywalled)

Physical limits and opposition

  • At least 47 large US data-center projects were delayed, withdrawn or blocked in 2026. Bernstein thinks only ~35% of the US pipeline is credible, and 1,000+ communities have tried moratoria. (FT)
  • Local resistance disrupted ~75 projects worth ~$130bn in Q1 alone. Amazon dropped nondisclosure agreements with local officials, and Google is announcing plans earlier, for example in Lea County, New Mexico. (WSJ)
  • Microsoft's Azure chief reportedly asked leaders to find 20% cost cuts. June-quarter free cash flow of $20bn was down ~20% y/y on data-center spending. Microsoft disputes the report. (The Information)

Chips

  • The selling was selective. Nvidia (P/E ~27) and TSMC held up better. SK Hynix fell ~20% on the week, and Samsung, Kioxia and SoftBank also dropped sharply on fears that memory prices have peaked and China's CXMT is catching up in DRAM. Broadcom (P/E ~42) was hurt after its CEO likened AI investing to a casino. (FT Unhedged)
  • Conflicting signals: Dell says the memory shortage will get worse next year, while the market is pricing a peak in the memory cycle. Both can be true: supply is tight now, and investors fear a downturn later.
  • China's draft export controls cover top AI models and agents plus chip-design, memory, packaging and networking technology. Open-weight models are mostly exempt below ~2tn parameters. China also ordered Meta's $2bn Manus deal unwound. (FT)
  • Nvidia released new open Nemotron models that Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Atlassian and Synopsys are customizing. Meta released an open model built to run locally on Macs in partnership with Apple. (WSJ)

AI agents and finance

  • Schwab CEO Rick Wurster calls a large AI-agent-driven shift of customers' idle cash "incredibly unlikely." Schwab is blocking third-party agents from customer accounts, and JPMorgan and Citi are doing the same. Schwab, Raymond James and LPL had each fallen ~6% when Meta's Muse agent launched. Schwab is piloting Claude connections for a small group of traders and posted record core net new assets of more than $190bn in Q3 (+38%). (WSJ, FT)
  • BoE Governor Bailey told the G30 that AI agents are a financial-stability issue, and the G7 plans simulated AI attacks on banks. (FT)
  • AI insurance gap: the largest AI companies face potential liabilities in the tens to hundreds of billions, but specialty coverage tops out around $50m (Munich Re). Insurers using standard Verisk policy forms can exclude AI, which Marsh opposes. This is a disclosure risk to watch for the Anthropic and OpenAI IPOs. (WSJ)

3. Global and political

  • Middle East: Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, including on Aramco's headquarters, and missiles intercepted near the Yanbu Red Sea hub, which matters more now that Iranian attacks on ships in Hormuz are rising. Fuel-cost protests are spreading across Europe, Asia and Latin America.
  • France: cost-of-living marches of ~550,000 people turned into the worst riots in years (~2,400 arrests). Le Pen leads ahead of the election in six months. French debt worries are spilling into French stocks, and the spread over German bonds is a risk. Italy also saw unrest.
  • Russia/Ukraine: Russia launched one of its largest strikes on Ukraine's grid (320+ drones). The US reportedly pressed Kyiv to stop hitting Russian refineries, and Ukraine launched its largest drone attack on St Petersburg.
  • Also on the calendar: Alberta independence referendum (Oct 19), Brazil presidential run-off, Israeli elections (Trump endorsed Netanyahu), the AfD surge in Germany, a US–EU dispute over Starlink spectrum, and Indonesia hit by a fresh MSCI warning.

4. What to watch next week

  1. Meta earnings (Wed): consensus revenue above $63bn with a margin just under 30%. Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon report the week after. These are the first big test of whether AI capex is paying off.
  2. FOMC, Oct 27–28: hold or hike. Watch Fed speakers and the PCE inflation data.
  3. Oil: escalation between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia, and Red Sea and Hormuz shipping.
  4. Credit: CoreWeave and other neocloud bonds and CDS, plus whether DayOne and Nscale actually price their IPOs.
  5. Europe: France's political response, the French–German bond spread, and the ECB and BoE.

5. Why it matters for you: IB interview talking points

  • ECM/DCM: Firmus is a textbook case of an IPO window closing. It failed on valuation, ~6× leverage and lock-ups. Banks lose fees, and issuers move to costlier private credit (Anthropic at ~10%).
  • Private credit and AI: debt-funded AI capex is the market's biggest fragility. Compare Dell, whose customers fund its inventory, with CoreWeave, which is heavily borrowed. That is a good capital-structure discussion.
  • Restructuring: distressed neocloud debt could become a pipeline of future mandates.
  • Boutique banks: Perella Weinberg's failed sale to Piper Sandler and Evercore naming co-presidents are both worth knowing for boutique interviews. (FT headlines)
  • Macro pitch: "Oil-driven inflation is keeping long-term rates above 5%, which exposes AI companies funded with debt. Big Tech earnings next week will show whether demand justifies the capex."
