Article Takeaways

Meta will sell up to $95bn of stock to pay for AI, and its own chip suppliers want in. $35bn of underwritten offerings now, then a $60bn at-the-market programme from Q1 2027. It's one of the largest stock sales ever.

  • Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom, all Meta suppliers, signalled interest in buying up to $5bn combined as cornerstone investors. Morgan Stanley and Goldman lead.
  • Why: 2026 capex is guided up to $145bn, and Meta has committed $800bn+ to data-centre and energy leases over the next decade. It says it needs "hundreds of billions" over several years.
  • Same day: SpaceX confirmed a $40bn, five-year, Apollo-led financing for Nvidia-powered AI servers, starting H1 2027. Alphabet raised $90bn of equity in June.
  • The catch: the sale comes with a new share structure to protect Zuckerberg's control, which could draw shareholder pushback (Meta dropped a similar plan in 2017). AI spending is eating free cash flow, and lenders are cooling on debt-funded capex.
  • Bearish: dilution and overhang for META. Bullish: order visibility for NVDA/AMD/AVGO, fees for MS/GS. But suppliers funding their own customer is vendor financing (inference). (FT)

French bonds rallied after Moody's held France at Aa3. The 10-year fell 0.13pp to 4.9%, about half of last week's selloff. Many had expected a downgrade.

  • The outlook stays negative, and Moody's assumes a deficit-cutting 2027 budget passes.
  • OAT–Bund spread 1.54pp, down from above 1.7pp last week (the widest since 2011). Italy's 10-year fell 0.09pp to 4.49%.
  • Brent fell 1.5% to just under $115 after a five-month high of $117 on Friday. Trump framed it as new strikes on Iran or a deal. Qatari officials went to Tehran, and Iran hosts Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials on Monday.
  • The catch: Iran stepped up tanker strikes in Hormuz last week, and the US pulled some diplomatic staff from Saudi Arabia over Houthi attacks. French politics aren't settled.
  • Bullish: euro-periphery bonds, French banks, and airlines if crude keeps easing (inference). (FT)

Central bank week: markets are pricing a tightening cycle while stocks wobble. The Fed decides Wednesday (Warsh's press conference), the ECB Thursday, the BoJ Friday.

  • Fed: ~9% odds of an October hike, but 2–3 quarter-point hikes priced by end-2027 (LSEG). September CPI came in hot.
  • Invesco argues hikes can't fix an oil and diesel supply shock and expects a steeper curve with more term premium. Warsh's light forward guidance adds volatility.
  • ECB: expected to hold at 2.5% after hikes in June and September. Odds of another hike this year are down to ~66% from fully priced earlier this month. Berenberg says there's no ECB backstop for France without policy fixes.
  • BoJ: a hold at 1.25% is expected after September's hike. Hawk Hajime Takata may dissent for a hike. The question is whether it signals December.
  • Read-through: a hawkish surprise from any of the three hits bonds and long-duration growth stocks; banks gain from a steeper curve (inference). (FT)

Bon Secours is buying Surgery Partners at a big premium, but below Bain's failed price. $23.50 a share in cash for a majority stake, a $5.7bn enterprise value including $3bn+ of debt. Friday's close was $15.75.

  • The shares jumped 46% in morning trading after being down 31% before the deal.
  • Bain, a holder since 2017, keeps its 39%. Its 2025 take-private at $6.7bn EV collapsed, so this clears at a lower value.
  • Surgery Partners runs 200+ surgery centres and hospitals in 30 states. Bon Secours runs 47 hospitals with ~$14bn of revenue.
  • Read-through: non-profit health systems are now a real exit for PE-backed outpatient assets (Ascension bought Amsurg for $3.9bn this year). Fees for Centerview, Leerink, Moelis and Jefferies (inference). (FT)

The Through-Line

AI's funding is moving from cash flow and bonds to equity and vendor money. Meta is selling up to $95bn of stock with its suppliers as buyers, and SpaceX is borrowing $40bn through Apollo for Nvidia servers. That lands in a week when three central banks may lean hawkish and oil sits near $115, so the cost of that capital isn't coming down (inference).
