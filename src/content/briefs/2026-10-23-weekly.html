Week ending Fri Oct 23, 2026 · WSJ, FT, The Information, this week's daily briefs (Oct 19–23), H100 and DRAM price log, AP/Labor Department data and Fed-odds trackers.

The week in one paragraph

Last week the AI trade cracked on debt. This week the question moved up the chain to the hyperscalers themselves. Early in the week SpaceX's AI-cloud quarter and Anthropic's draft S-1 (H1 revenue ~$16.3B, 15x a year earlier) lifted chip stocks. Then on Wednesday Meta said capital is getting scarce, that it will need "hundreds of billions" over several years, and that it is creating nonvoting shares to raise it. Thursday's selloff took $124bn off Meta, ~6.7% off the chip index and sent the 10-year below 5% in its biggest one-day rally since March 2023. Friday's worry was oil instead. Brent hit $117 on bets that the Iran war escalates after the midterms, which pushed inflation expectations and yields back up. Net for the week: S&P 500 −0.5%, Nasdaq −0.7%, Dow +0.4%, Russell 2000 +2%. Rotation, not panic. Small caps and staples gained, AI and gold lost.

Article Takeaways

Meta said the quiet part: capital for AI is getting scarce. Its filing warned of an "increasingly challenging environment for raising capital" as Alphabet, SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI all raise at once, so it will raise early. Future commitments rose to ~$825bn from ~$700bn in July.

  • Q3 revenue $64.6bn (+26%, above the $61–64bn guide). Free cash flow was −$2.1bn, its first negative quarter in at least a decade. Q3 capex $35bn; up to $145bn for the year.
  • Shares −7% Thursday (−$124bn). Meta's 5-year CDS rose ~10bp to ~116bp, the highest since it started trading.
  • New nonvoting share class via a 2-for-1 split, trading from January. It lets Meta sell stock without diluting Zuckerberg (~61% of votes). He can sell up to half his stake and committed to stay in California for eight years.
  • Bearish: anyone assuming hyperscalers can self-fund capex. Bullish: ECM and DCM desks; Meta has become a serial issuer (inference). (WSJ, FT)

Thursday's AI rout sent money into Treasuries. The 10-year fell ~0.16–0.19pp to ~4.95%, its biggest one-day drop since March 2023. A Barclays strategist tied it to "potential cracks in the AI trade," not to data.

  • SpaceX −9.1%, Tesla −3.1%, cooling names like Vertiv lower. Staples (Clorox +4.6%) and regional banks rose.
  • CoreWeave 2032 bonds held just under 80c, down from ~90c last month. The financing squeeze is spreading from neoclouds to investment-grade tech spreads (Oracle, SpaceX, Meta widened again Friday).
  • Read-through: a slower capex pace would cool growth and inflation, which helps bonds and reduces pressure on the Fed (inference). (WSJ, FT)

Oil is now the second shock: Brent hit $117, the highest since April. It touched $117.45 Friday and settled at $116.72, up more than 10% in October. Iran stepped up tanker strikes in Hormuz, and the US pulled some staff from Saudi Arabia over Houthi attacks.

  • Brent for April 2027 delivery trades above $100, so the market expects high prices to last. Diesel is ~$200/bbl.
  • Rapidan sees ~$150 if Gulf infrastructure is hit. US 5-year breakevens rose 0.08pp to 2.47% on Friday.
  • Bullish: oil majors (report next week), energy's share of S&P earnings growth. Bearish: consumers, airlines, rate-cut hopes. (FT)

S&P 500 earnings are booming, but two chipmakers carry a third of it. FactSet sees Q3 earnings +30% y/y. Nvidia and Micron alone are 34% of that growth, and ten companies are about two-thirds.

  • Micron's last quarterly profit rose 1,078%; Nvidia is expected at about +90%.
  • Ex-top 10, the S&P still grows ~10%, so the base isn't broken.
  • The catch: the index's profit story rests on the same AI capex that Meta just said is hard to fund. (WSJ)

Musk wants to merge Tesla into SpaceX, the largest deal ever attempted. Both boards set up committees. Combined value is ~$3.5tn; SpaceX would issue shares to buy Tesla, leaving Musk with majority control (85% of SpaceX votes vs 20% of Tesla today).

  • SpaceX revenue more than doubled in Q3 on renting out AI compute. Musk lifted his 2027 data-center plan to 10GW, roughly $500bn at ~$50bn/GW.
  • SpaceX sells compute on short contracts that reset often, a bet that compute prices keep rising. Executives say new contracts pay back the build in under a year.
  • Bearish: conglomerate discount, governance (potential pay up to ~$1.7tn), Tesla's weak auto business. Needs a Tesla shareholder vote and global approvals. (FT)

Anthropic's S-1 shows the fastest revenue growth of any IPO candidate, and the biggest compute bill. H1 2026 revenue was ~$16.3B vs $1.1B a year earlier, with $600B+ of compute commitments. An IPO is expected as soon as next month.

  • The Information says quarterly growth slowed to 45% in Q3. OpenAI is offering 30–40% discounts to win back coders: Rippling moved from 57% Anthropic / 16% OpenAI in May to 52% OpenAI / 18% Anthropic.
  • Anthropic still has the better margin: Q2 compute ~$5.6B vs $11.5B revenue. OpenAI is buying share with already-negative margins.
  • OpenRouter says Anthropic retook the lead after Claude Opus 5.5. Switching costs are hours, so pricing power is fragile. (WSJ, The Information)

OpenAI cut its round to a $1.25tn valuation. It aims to close ~$30B by Oct 30, below the $1.4tn pitched earlier this month but ~50% above its current $852B mark.

  • With Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX all tapping the same investors, Meta's "scarce capital" warning is the same story seen from the buyer's side. (FT, The Information)

The AI-infrastructure IPO window stayed shut. SK Hynix dropped the US IPO of Solidigm "in light of recent capital market conditions." SB Energy pushed its IPO to December or 2027 and wants to see how Anthropic trades. Nvidia moved to rescue Firmus after its IPO flopped.

  • Nvidia has >$170B on the line for its own customers (up to $164.5B of guarantees and commitments plus >$7B invested in AI clouds).
  • Bearish: Nvidia's reported demand is partly self-financed. Bullish: private credit and venture lenders fill the gap at higher rates. (FT, The Information)

VC firms are borrowing to keep up with AI rounds. Firms are using LP co-investment pools and JPMorgan warehouse loans to fund rounds before they raise the money.

  • Rounds over $100M were 87% of VC dollars in H1 (PitchBook). B Capital put $100M into Reflection AI through an LP pool at 0% management fee and 10% carry.
  • Leverage is moving into venture, the least liquid part of the AI stack (inference). (The Information)

France's 10-year topped 5% for the first time since 2002. It reached 5.08% Thursday. The spread over Bunds topped 1.7pp, the highest since 2011; Italy's widened to 1.2pp and Belgium's to 1.1pp.

  • Moody's kept France at Aa3 on Friday, assuming a deficit-cutting 2027 budget passes.
  • The ECB is expected to hold at 2.5% Thursday. Berenberg doubts it will buy French bonds without fiscal reform. (FT, WSJ)

Instacart is exploring a sale. It is valued at $12bn and has talked with DoorDash and Uber (Uber talks aren't ongoing). A normal 30–40% premium implies ~$16–17bn. Shares rose 10% Friday.

  • Antitrust is the main hurdle for any rival buyer. (The Information)

AI-found bugs are now a systemic cyber risk. PHP, which runs up to 70% of websites, patched "PHP2Shell," found with OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol. Some government sites went offline pre-emptively.

  • Separately, a hacking group used agents built on Claude, Grok and Moonshot's Kimi to automate attacks on 400+ Latin American financial targets (Straiker).
  • Bullish: security vendors (Datadog's ~$4bn Upwind bid last week fits). Bearish: banks' and insurers' cyber loss assumptions. (WSJ, The Information)

The Through-Line

The AI boom moved from an equity story to a funding story. Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX and the neoclouds all need outside capital at the same time, while oil above $115 keeps inflation and rates high. That's why tech fell and Treasuries rallied together on Thursday, then both reversed on Friday's oil move. Demand isn't the problem: H100 rents rose 15% on the week and memory prices kept edging up. The price and availability of money is (inference).

Top Takeaways

  1. Meta said rivals' fundraising is making capital scarce and that it will need "hundreds of billions" over several years, then created nonvoting stock to raise it. Hyperscalers are moving from self-funding to market-funding capex, so their cost of capital now depends on the same bond and equity window that just closed on neoclouds. (META −6.98% Thu)
  2. Thursday's selloff sent the 10-year down ~0.19pp, its biggest drop since March 2023, while the chip index fell 6.7%. Investors are swapping AI equity risk for duration, because slower AI capex would mean less growth and less inflation. (NVDA −0.59% Fri; SOX −6.7% Thu)
  3. Brent hit $117, its highest since April, as traders priced a post-midterm escalation with Iran, and the curve shows >$100 into April 2027. A longer oil shock lifts inflation expectations (5-year breakevens 2.47%) and keeps the Fed leaning toward hikes. (no single ticker; Brent ~+6% on the week)
  4. Two chipmakers account for 34% of expected S&P 500 Q3 earnings growth of ~30%. Index earnings are now a leveraged bet on AI capex, so any capex slowdown hits EPS estimates and the multiple at once. (MU, NVDA; NVDA −0.59% Fri)
  5. Musk set up a Tesla–SpaceX merger review, a ~$3.5tn combination where SpaceX would issue stock to buy Tesla. Musk wants SpaceX's AI-compute cash flows and his majority control to fund a 10GW, ~$500bn data-center plan, while Tesla shareholders get exposure to a better-performing business. (SPCX −9.1% Thu; TSLA −3.1% Thu)
  6. Anthropic's draft S-1 shows H1 revenue of ~$16.3B, 15x a year earlier, but quarterly growth slowed to 45% as OpenAI discounts 30–40% to win coding customers. AI labs compete on price because switching takes hours, which caps margins just as both prepare to raise record sums. (no public ticker)
  7. SK Hynix dropped Solidigm's US IPO and SB Energy delayed its listing to December or later. AI-hardware issuers can't get equity at the prices they want, so funding shifts to private credit, vendor financing (Nvidia's >$170B) and balance sheets. (000660.KS move not found)
  8. France's 10-year hit 5.08%, its highest since 2002, with the Bund spread above 1.7pp. Foreign holders are selling and the ECB has signalled no rescue without fiscal reform, so euro-area sovereign risk is back on the table. Moody's held its Aa3 rating Friday. (no single ticker)
  9. Instacart is exploring a sale after talks with DoorDash and Uber, with a 30–40% premium implying ~$16–17bn. Slower growth (11% in 2024–25) and a strong cash position make it a clean strategic target, but antitrust limits the buyer list. (CART +10% Fri)
  10. PHP patched a flaw affecting up to 70% of websites that AI found, and hackers used Claude and Grok agents to automate attacks on banks. AI cuts the cost of attack faster than the cost of defense, which pushes up security budgets and M&A valuations for security firms. (no single ticker)

Conflicting signals

  • AI demand vs AI funding: H100 rents +15% and memory up, while Meta, SK Hynix and SB Energy can't raise equity on their terms. Demand is fine; capital is the constraint.
  • Inflation vs growth: oil and breakevens point to Fed hikes; the AI capex wobble and Treasury rally point to slower growth. Futures price only ~7–9% odds of an October hike but two to three hikes by end-2027.
  • Havens: Treasuries rallied Thursday but gold fell 3.3%, its first break below $4,000 since July. Gold is trading like a risk asset.
  • Labour market: jobless claims at a three-month low (194K) don't fit fears of an AI-driven slowdown yet.
  • Breadth: Russell 2000 +2% and Dow up vs Nasdaq down. Rotation, not a broad selloff.

IB interview talking points

  • One-sentence macro pitch: "The AI build-out has become a capital-markets story: Meta now says capital is scarce, the IPO window has closed for AI infrastructure, and oil at $117 keeps rates high, so the winners are whoever can fund capex cheaply — and the bankers who arrange that funding."
  • ECM: Meta's nonvoting share class is a new way to sell equity without diluting founder control (compare Cloudflare's Delaware fight). Anthropic and OpenAI could be the two largest IPOs ever, while Solidigm and SB Energy show the window shutting for hardware.
