Monday, Oct 19, 2026 · FT, WSJ, The Information, prices

Article Takeaways

America's AI data-centre boom is fraying at the financing layer. At least 47 large US projects were delayed, withdrawn or blocked this year. Bernstein thinks only ~35% of the planned pipeline is credible.

  • A CoreWeave unsecured bond went distressed last week. A $1.1bn bond for a CoreWeave-leased Virginia site is down ~5% three weeks after pricing.
  • IPOs are stalling. SB Energy shelved a $50bn-valuation listing; Firmus and OpenAI delayed theirs. Nebius needs ~$250bn for 5GW and has raised ~20%.
  • The catch: tenants hold walk-away clauses for late delivery. Anthropic can exit its $45bn Nscale deal if West Virginia timelines slip.
  • Bearish: leveraged neoclouds and ex-miners (CRWV, NBIS, CIFR, WULF) and their lenders. Bullish: labs and hyperscalers, which may get better terms (inference). (FT)

Microsoft walked from Nscale's flagship UK site because it has no power. It cancelled the full 50MW lease at Loughton (~23,000 GB300s) after grid power slipped to 2030 or later.

  • Earlier, MSFT's LOI for up to 1.35GW at Nscale's West Virginia site disappeared from Nscale's draft IPO documents.
  • Nscale had 25k chips online in August against ~500k planned. It still targets a $35bn NY IPO.
  • The UK grid queue has 315 data centres seeking ~73GW. National peak demand is 45GW. Power, not chips, is the binding constraint. (FT)

AI credit is splitting: asset-backed bonds are holding, neocloud corporate bonds are not. CoreWeave's 2030 notes yield close to 15%. A bond on an Applied Digital site leased to CoreWeave yields ~8%.

  • Data-centre issuers have sold >$100B of USD bonds YTD, ~25% of AI-related issuance (vs ~16% last year).
  • Secured, amortising structures, and leases backed by IG tenants like Meta, protect bondholders.
  • Pimco: AI credit is "not one big trade." (WSJ)

Nvidia is more exposed to a neocloud shakeout than hyperscaler capex suggests. Non-hyperscaler data-centre revenue was $40B in the July quarter, 45% of the segment and more than double a year earlier.

  • Executives expect 7–10 neoclouds to survive, against hundreds today. Gartner: 80% lose independence by 2030.
  • Nvidia has signed $20B of leases to pass to neoclouds and holds a $6.3B CoreWeave rent-back backstop.
  • The key split: lessors like CoreWeave can fail without stalling the build-out, but failed builders (Nebius, IREN, Nscale) cut Nvidia shipments directly. (The Information)

Nvidia wants all HBM base dies made on TSMC 3nm. It asked Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron to build its custom "NVHBM" base dies at TSMC and to use sixth-gen DRAM for the core dies. Samsung agreed, its first time working with TSMC at the HBM stage.

  • Bullish TSMC (logic content in every stack). Narrows HBM makers' room to differentiate (inference).
  • Second-hand report via Korean press. (The Information)

Blue Owl's private-credit franchise is still bleeding. Clients asked to pull 17% of assets from its largest fund last quarter. Stock is ~67% off its Jan 2025 high.

  • The fund it restructured has ~10% of remaining loans in default. A related insurer, Kuvare, bought some of the loans sold in February.
  • Retail inflows are down ~60%. Bankers have pitched the founders on a full or partial sale.
  • Its growth pivot is AI data-centre financing, which ties it to the same credit risk as above (inference). (WSJ)

The BoE is probing whether Barclays understated private-credit risk capital. An insider says traders booked total return swaps on illiquid private-credit loans in the trading book, where they need less capital.

  • The PRA told banks last year that repackaging illiquid assets isn't enough for trading-book treatment.
  • The PRA CEO came from Barclays and is recused until year-end. (FT)

Anthropic's trust is the governance catch in a possible $100B IPO. WSJ reported a raise of up to $100B at a valuation near $2T. Anthropic will also put 5% of its shares into a "public benefit" fund.

  • The trust appoints 4 of 7 directors and gets 5 days' notice before non-routine debt, a sale of 50% or more, or control transfers of frontier models.
  • Public holders get few governance rights. Demand for AI exposure likely outweighs that (inference). (WSJ)

China wants approval rights over its top AI models leaving the country. A MOFCOM draft adds 23 AI technologies to the restricted-export list (LLMs, agents, chip design, clusters). Top-10-ranked models would need approval.

  • Already-public and open-source work is exempt. Comments are due Nov 15. (The Information)

Accenture is shopping its China unit for up to $1.5bn. JPMorgan is running the review after unsolicited interest. Chinese data rules block cross-border synergies.

  • Investor day Wednesday: subdued growth for two years, then AI-led acceleration. (FT)

China's Q3 growth beat on exports, not consumers. GDP rose 4.5% y/y (vs 4.4% expected). September exports rose 36% on AI demand.

  • Fixed-asset investment is −7.2% YTD and the property slump is in its fifth year. The EU is adding safeguards against Chinese imports. (FT)

Markets are bouncing after a 5.5% Nasdaq 100 week, the worst since April 2025. Futures are up and the Kospi rose 1.8% after falling 11% last week.

  • It looks like relief, not a reversal; the AI credit question is still open (inference). (FT)

Monday premarket: semis bounce, CXMT and French banks drop. Intel is up 3%+ after −5.8% Friday. Chinese DRAM maker CXMT fell 8% despite strong guidance and a buyback. SocGen, BNP and Crédit Agricole are down ~3% after weekend riots in France. (WSJ)

The Through-Line

Every AI story today turns on who carries the risk if data centres arrive late or never. Labs and hyperscalers have cancel clauses, and Microsoft just used one. Neoclouds, asset managers and banks that funded them hold fixed debt and hard deadlines. Nvidia sits in between through leases and backstops. Watch credit spreads and grid connections more than GPU orders.

Top Takeaways

  1. CoreWeave's 2030 notes now yield close to 15% and one of its unsecured bonds went distressed. A secured bond on a site it leases yields only ~8%. Lenders are pricing the data centre above the neocloud renting it, because secured, amortising debt gets repaid even if the tenant fails. Expect neoclouds' funding costs to keep rising. (CRWV −11.91%)
  2. Microsoft cancelled the full 50MW Loughton lease with Nscale once grid power slipped to 2030 or later. Delivery risk sits with the builder because tenants hold walk-away clauses. In the UK, 73GW of requests chase a 45GW grid, so power, not chips, now sets the pace of the build-out. (MSFT −1.93%)
  3. Nvidia's non-hyperscaler data-centre revenue hit $40B, 45% of the segment, and executives expect only 7–10 neoclouds to survive. Nvidia props up that demand with $20B of pass-through leases and a $6.3B CoreWeave backstop. If a builder rather than a lessor fails, shipments fall directly. (NVDA −1.43%)
  4. Nvidia told Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron to make its custom HBM base dies on TSMC 3nm, and Samsung agreed. Standardising specs simplifies validation, moves logic value to TSMC, and narrows memory makers' room to differentiate (inference). (TSM −2.28%)
  5. Chinese DRAM maker CXMT fell 8% despite strong guidance and a buyback. TrendForce still sees 4Q26 contract prices rising on AI server demand. Investors are fading the memory cycle even with pricing intact, a sentiment risk for MU, SK Hynix and Samsung (inference). (688825.SS −8%)
  6. Blue Owl clients asked to pull 17% of its largest private-credit fund, and bankers have pitched a sale of the firm. Retail inflows fell ~60%, and its growth plan is data-centre financing. That puts it on the same credit risk now hitting neoclouds. (OWL, Friday move not found)
  7. The PRA is examining a claim that Barclays booked TRS on illiquid private-credit loans in the trading book. That would flatter capital, because trading-book exposure needs less of it. A ruling against that treatment would raise the capital cost of bank lending to the ~$2tn private-credit market. (BARC.L −1.5% early Monday)
  8. Anthropic may raise up to $100B at a valuation near $2T, but its Long-Term Benefit Trust names 4 of 7 directors and must get notice before a major sale or debt. Public buyers get growth with limited control, the SpaceX template, so the governance discount may stay small (inference). (no public ticker)
  9. China proposed licensing exports of 23 AI technologies, including top-10-ranked models and chip design, effective now with comments until Nov 15. Foreign licensing and M&A of Chinese AI firms get harder, and open-source releases become the main path out (inference). (no public ticker)
