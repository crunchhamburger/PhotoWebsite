Article Takeaways

Democrats won both chambers of Congress, and markets turned a sell-off into a rally. Futures fell overnight as results came in, then reversed. The Dow opened up ~900–1,000 points and the Nasdaq was up 1%+.

  • Yields and the dollar fell. The bull case: divided government could mean lower inflation and rates if Trump steps back from more Middle East conflict.
  • Oil fell ~$2. Traders put fair value near $90/bbl now that Hormuz exports are almost back to normal, though some risk premium remains.
  • Musk spent almost $0.5bn backing Republicans. SpaceX was ~−7% and Tesla ~−5% premarket.
  • Treasury kept auction sizes unchanged but softened its promise on how long they stay there, which could mean bigger auctions later.
  • Bullish: renewables (Vestas, Orsted, First Solar), rate-sensitive stocks, European banks. Bearish: defense, Musk companies, Trump Media. (WSJ, FT)

Data centers lost at the ballot box. Governor candidates promising statewide pauses on data centers won, including Gina Hinojosa over Greg Abbott in Texas, and many local moratoriums passed.

  • Georgia matters most: Atlanta is the largest data-center construction market (CBRE). The Information said pause candidates could win in up to 15 states.
  • Nvidia and Micron fell more than 1% on worries about data-center restrictions.
  • Bearish: permitting risk for hyperscaler capex and for power and AI-capex suppliers in those states (inference). (WSJ, The Information, FT)

AMD beat because data centers are now nearly two-thirds of its business. Q3 revenue $13.4bn vs $13.1bn expected (+45% y/y); data-center revenue nearly doubled to $8.5bn, 64% of sales vs 47% a year ago. Q4 guide ~$17bn vs $16.23bn expected.

  • Non-GAAP net income $3.5bn vs $3.2bn; gross margin 56%, in line.
  • The catch: a $1.1bn charge for OpenAI's warrants (up to 160M shares, ~10%, at $0.01, tied to up to 6GW of MI450). OpenAI hasn't exercised yet.
  • Stock +3.65% Tuesday, then +7–9% after hours. Still −23% over the past month.
  • Bullish: AI GPU demand is holding up. Bearish: Intel looks worse by comparison; full warrant exercise dilutes holders ~10% (inference). (WSJ)

Anthropic's IPO investors want a cheaper price: ~$1.5–1.6tn, not ~$2tn. Their reasons are choppy markets, too much AI in portfolios, the data-center backlash, and slow hyperscaler AI profits.

  • Roadshow as soon as next week; listing the week before Thanksgiving, with a price range.
  • Updated S-1: Q3 revenue $17bn vs $11.6bn in Q2. Compute commitments ~$895.4bn: ~$447.5bn through 2030, the rest 2031–2045.
  • Some expect conservative pricing for a first-day pop. Other IPO candidates are waiting on its debut.
  • Bullish: a cheaper price supports a pop and the 2027 IPO pipeline. Bearish: suppliers carry long-dated counterparty risk on those commitments (inference). (WSJ, FT)

Apollo now finances more AI chips than any neocloud. $40bn of Nvidia Vera Rubin chips leased to SpaceX, on top of June's $35bn raise (with Broadcom and Blackstone) for Google TPUs leased to Anthropic: ~$75bn in total, behind only the hyperscalers.

  • That beats the 100+ neocloud chip-financing deals since 2023 that The Information tallied.
  • Excluded: last week's $63bn Citi/BofA/Blackstone financing of Anthropic's Broadcom chips.
  • The catch: it's insurance money, so insurers carry chip residual-value risk and exposure to two big lessees (Musk, Anthropic).
  • Bullish: APO and BX fees. Bearish: neoclouds like CoreWeave lose their funding edge (inference). (The Information)

Washington is pulling US AI infrastructure away from Chinese customers. Oracle cancelled a $7bn AI deal with Tencent after Trump administration pressure. Nvidia told cloud providers that most of their clients must be non-Chinese.

  • Earlier reports described the Tencent deal as a 5-year lease of ~100,000 chips in Southeast Asian data centers.
  • Bearish: Oracle backlog and China demand routed through offshore clouds (inference). (FT, headlines only)

Apple warned that memory chip costs will hit margins. It called the costs "unprecedented", and the warning overshadowed a strong holiday-quarter sales forecast. Shares −1.9% Tuesday.

  • Bullish: memory makers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) have pricing power. Bearish: device makers absorb it (inference). (FT, headline only)

Beijing stopped taking new IPO applications after AI worries hit Chinese stocks. The CSRC will process deals already filed. Big holders were asked to hold off on large share sales.

  • CXMT closed at ¥38.80, down from ¥61.80 in August, and proposed a buyback of up to ~$300mn. The Shanghai Composite fell 9%+ over two months.
  • Support: the PBoC ran >$10bn of liquidity operations to fund stock buying and cut a bank lending rate to 1% from 1.25%. Buybacks below the IPO price are now allowed.
  • DeepSeek and YMTC reviews continue, but neither will rush into a weak market.
  • Read-through: fewer new A-shares near term; Chinese issuers may look to Hong Kong and the US (inference). (WSJ)

Companies are rushing to do deals while antitrust stays loose. YTD announced M&A is $2.6tn, +66% y/y (Dealogic). Salesforce has spent at least $25bn on 8 deals in 12 months, almost all cash, including Cresta (~$2.7bn reported) and Braze ($5.7bn).

  • Funded by a 35% FCF margin (FY26, up from 20% in FY23), >$14bn a year.
  • The catch: Benioff's last deal spree hurt margins and drew activists.
  • Also: Shopify −7.6% after AI spending hit subscription gross margin. (The Information)

The Through-Line

Politics is now a direct input to the AI build-out. Voters backed data-center pauses in big construction states, Washington is pushing Oracle and Nvidia away from Chinese customers, and Beijing has closed its IPO window. Demand isn't slowing yet: AMD's data-center sales nearly doubled, H100 rental prices hit a three-month high, and Apple is paying more for memory. That leaves the constraint at permits and financing, and private credit like Apollo's plus a cheaper Anthropic IPO are filling the gap (inference).
