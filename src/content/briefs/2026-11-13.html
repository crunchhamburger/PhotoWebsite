Article Takeaways

Jensen Huang is out, and the CFO who fought his financing deals now runs Nvidia. Huang resigned Thursday after 33 years over two extramarital affairs, one with Megaspeed's Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese spy, and one with an Nvidia employee. Colette Kress, CFO for 13 years, is interim CEO, and director Harvey Jones becomes Nvidia's first chair.

  • The board will weigh internal and external candidates. Kress holds an all-hands on Nov 18; results are due Tuesday, Nov 17.
  • In an August meeting Huang called Kress "a deer in the headlights". The meeting was about giving billions of dollars of guarantees to companies that couldn't afford Nvidia's chips.
  • That credit-support program (guarantees to neoclouds and data-center developers for a cut of their revenue) was run by Huang's son-in-law Nico Caprez and VP Raj Mirpuri, with dozens of companies lined up. Kress questioned how deals were picked, valued and measured.
  • OpenAI's Ohio campus is the example: after WSJ reported a possible $250B Nvidia backstop on Jul 26, NVDA fell 5% (about $250B of value). Kress reworked it and halved Nvidia's initial commitment before signing on Aug 17. Some deals were paused in late August and now need executive-team and board sign-off.
  • Huang told the finance team in August that big cloud providers were moving to alternative chips while Nvidia's committed wafer and memory purchases were piling up. By October he planned to ration supply and hold prices.
  • Read-through: slower, more disciplined financing. Negative for neoclouds that depend on guarantees; the purchase commitments are a risk if hyperscalers keep diversifying (inference).

(WSJ; FT)

The market sold Nvidia's dependents, not AI. NVDA fell as much as 14% intraday Thursday and closed −4.34% (FT has about −5%), down 31% from its Oct 6 record, with more than $2T of value gone at one point.

  • Losers: CoreWeave and Iren about −7%, Super Micro −11.30% (also a Bloomberg report of a DOJ examination), Nebius −16.84% (its own capex raise and delayed revenue target). Winners: AMD +5.86% (William Blair upgrade), Broadcom +5.54%, Arista +5.1%. Hyperscalers closed higher.
  • Indexes barely moved: S&P 500 −0.35% to 7,592, Nasdaq −0.39%, Dow −0.36%. Small caps did worse, Russell 2000 −1.66%.
  • Retail investors bought $111M of NVDA in the first half hour (Vanda), a record for that window. Oppenheimer removed its price target, Bernstein halved its target, and Wedbush couldn't recall a comparable founder exit with no succession plan. Baird sees no near-term change to the business.
  • Trump publicly called for Tim Cook to replace Huang. The Senate Banking Committee has asked Huang to testify next month.
  • The catch: the variable that matters is whether Nvidia keeps using its balance sheet to backstop customers. Kress oversaw that expansion but also cut back the OpenAI deal.

(WSJ; price notes)

Nvidia's cheque book is the real exposure: $124bn of equity bets plus tens of billions of guarantees. FT counts $124bn of equity investments including commitments at end-July and 120+ investments this year, about one every 2.5 days (PitchBook).

  • Dealroom's split: $35bn of planned acquisitions (Hugging Face, Groq), $23bn of listed stakes (Intel, Nokia), $60bn+ into 200+ start-ups (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI).
  • Guarantees: up to $105bn of credit support for OpenAI's lease and power payments at the 4.25GW Ohio campus, in exchange for 20 years of Nvidia-only hardware; $36bn committed to buy compute from AI clouds that can't find customers (contracts they borrow against); $20bn of data-center leases to pass on, including a 1GW Texas campus part-sublet to Lambda.
  • Still planned: $10bn into Anthropic alongside its IPO next week, and a scheme with Wall Street to mobilise $500bn+ of outside capital for chip financing.
  • Most exposed are companies mid-raise (The Information): Firmus pulled its IPO and wants at least $2B privately; Nscale's guarantees (likely over $5B) on a 460MW site were unsigned as of late October; Lambda is in a final pre-IPO round with nearly $20B of leases guaranteed. Seen as safe because signed: Safe Superintelligence ($5B) and Thinking Machines (about $2.5B).
  • The catch: a neocloud executive expects Nvidia dealmaking to mostly halt until this settles, and AMD and Broadcom can't replace the guarantee capacity. The two sources size the spree differently: FT has $124bn of equity at end-July, The Information $140B of deals including guarantees in just two months. Different scopes, not a contradiction.

(FT; The Information)

Nscale is the first test case: it now says it needs more cash before its $35bn IPO. An updated SEC filing Thursday says it must find additional financing to meet 2027 obligations after a large Nvidia chip order for its $45bn Anthropic contract, and some of what it owes can't be deferred, reduced or cancelled. September's filing said it had enough for at least 12 months.

  • Nine months to September (estimated): net loss of almost $1.7bn on $307mn of revenue. Debt about $5.5bn.
  • The Anthropic deal covers the Monarch campus in West Virginia. Nscale has six months to finance the data center and GPUs and hasn't done so for the first site. Talks on a second building must finish by end-November, after which Anthropic has no outstanding obligation.
  • Microsoft pulled out of a $3.2bn London-area contract (no grid connection until the 2030s) and declined a Texas option. Figure took part of Texas in a $3.5bn deal, half paid in Figure shares.
  • CoreWeave is down about 30% in a month and Nebius about 40%. Negative for the neocloud IPO window and debt-funded AI capacity (inference).

(FT; WSJ; The Information)

Huang's $135bn of stock is a supply overhang until his plans and the legal position are clear. He holds about 812mn shares worth $135bn at Thursday's close, under 5% of Nvidia; his foundation holds about 59mn more (over $9bn).

  • He has sold only about $3bn since the 1999 IPO and nothing this year. No margin-loan risk: Nvidia bars executives from pledging stock.
  • Tuesday's results could open a selling window, but investigations into his relationship with an executive at a major customer could restrict trading. William Blair calls it a significant near-term overhang.
  • 812mn shares is about six days of Nvidia's total volume, so an exit would be staggered over months. Over $100mn of unvested stock may be forfeited.
  • The benign precedent is Steve Wynn in 2018: $2.1bn, mostly sold privately to two institutions (inference).

(FT)

Companies tied to Alice Huang's family bought into DeepSeek to win its business for banned Nvidia chips. The Information, citing three people: Century Litong and its parent Lettall invested in DeepSeek's second round through other investors' vehicles, aiming to supply Blackwell chips that can't legally be sold to China.

  • The round raised 105.5B yuan ($15.8B) at about a 615.5B yuan ($92B) valuation, the largest Chinese AI raise so far; Tencent and CATL were the biggest investors.
  • Century Litong became an Nvidia preferred cloud partner four months after its 2023 founding and has imported 240,000 Nvidia chips worth at least $7.5B into China since then, including Blackwell after the smuggling crackdown.
  • Lettall put 95M yuan ($14M) into one vehicle; the total is unknown. Alice Huang holds no stake or title; the links run through relatives.
  • DeepSeek is adapting to Huawei chips but still wants Blackwell. More evidence for tighter export controls and enforcement; supportive of Huawei as the domestic alternative (inference).

(The Information)

ByteDance is buying back stock at a $400B valuation while AI spending cuts into profit. Up to $6B from existing investors at $241.35 a share versus $200.41 last November, a 20% higher mark.

  • H1 net profit fell by a single-digit percentage to $20B because of AI spending.
  • January's deal sold 80% of TikTok's US data-security operation to an American-owned JV and kept the revenue-generating business, removing the ban risk. An IPO still needs Beijing's approval and has no date.
  • ByteDance wants to push outside shareholders' right to force a repurchase from Dec 11 this year to Dec 11, 2030. It will give AI staff 300 million shares tracking the AI business to counter poaching.

(The Information)

Treasuries had their worst week since April 2025, and they stopped hedging tech. The 10-year reached 5.17% Friday, up 0.31 points on the week; the 2-year rose 0.37 points to 4.89%, its biggest weekly rise since late 2024.

  • Trigger: October CPI at +0.4% m/m on Tuesday. A 25bp Fed hike in December is fully priced with about a 13% chance of 50bp; a second in January is largely priced (10% odds a week ago); a third by June 2027. Waller floated 50bp.
  • Active managers who bought Treasuries over the previous two weeks were forced out.
  • WSJ's Mackintosh: from the Oct 6 Nasdaq peak until this week, 10-year yields tracked CoreWeave's share price (10-day correlation up to 83%) and ignored strong data: Citi's surprise index hit a three-year high and GDPNow has Q4 at 3.5%. He estimates a quarter to half of growth comes from AI capex plus Nasdaq wealth effects.
  • The two pieces differ on the hedge: FT notes bonds fell with tech this week, while Mackintosh still likes Treasurys against another AI crash.
  • A 10-year above 5% raises funding costs for AI build-out borrowers just as Nvidia's support becomes less certain (inference).

(FT; WSJ)

Oil has swung to a surplus for the first time since the US-Iran war began. The IEA says supply beat demand by almost 2mn b/d in October; the deficit had peaked at 6.3mn b/d.

  • It sees the surplus reaching 4.5mn b/d in Q2 2027, the largest in seven years, and 2026 demand down 2.7mn b/d, not back to 2025 levels until 2028.
  • Brent is about $97, down 18% this month; Dated Brent about $95 from $136 in early October. Pont Capital's Christyan Malek sees $50–$70 if the conflict doesn't escalate.
  • The catch: jet fuel and diesel stay tight on refining limits and Hormuz risk, Aramco's CEO says restocking could take two years, and flows depend on US naval escorts.
  • Helps headline inflation at the margin; negative for upstream producers, less so for refiners (inference).

The Through-Line

Huang's exit matters less as a personnel story than as a financing one. Nvidia's guarantees, equity cheques and capacity backstops were the credit support under neoclouds and AI start-ups, and the person now in charge is the one who tried to slow them. That pullback is arriving in the same week the 10-year went back above 5% and Nscale admitted a funding gap, so the cost of AI capacity is rising from both sides at once. The market's answer so far is to separate companies that needed Nvidia's balance sheet from those with their own (inference).
