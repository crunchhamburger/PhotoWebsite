Article Takeaways

Marvell is buying Lumentum for ~$115bn because AI networking is moving to optics. All-stock: 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share, ~$1,132 a share, a ~24% premium. Equity value ~$102.5bn (Reuters); the largest deal of 2026.

  • Lumentum holders end up with ~1/3 of the company. Marvell targets >$1bn of annual cost synergies and pitches a $550bn combined market.
  • The catch: close isn't expected until 1H 2028, a long antitrust window (China's SAMR likely, inference). Marvell fell ~13% on the first deal reports Oct 29 and was ~−3% premarket today.
  • Nvidia had already put $2bn into each company.
  • Bullish: optical interconnect as the next AI bottleneck; TrendForce sees AI networking at $279.8bn in 2027. Bearish: Coherent and Broadcom's optics face a bigger rival; Marvell holders carry dilution and deal risk (inference). (The Information, FT)

Nvidia may guarantee >$5bn of Nscale's debt so the Anthropic campus gets built. The project is a ~460MW West Virginia campus under a ~$45bn, ~6-year Anthropic contract; Anthropic's IPO filing reportedly lists ~$44.6bn of Nscale commitments.

  • Nvidia would be chip seller, equity investor and credit backstop in the same chain. If Nscale defaults, Nvidia pays.
  • Not signed; terms could change. It follows Anthropic's $35bn deal with Lambda, another Nvidia-backed neocloud.
  • Read-through: neocloud credit risk is migrating onto Nvidia's balance sheet, which revives the "circular financing" critique (inference). (The Information)

Anthropic's revenue hit ~$17bn in Q3, but it swung to an operating loss right before its IPO. Revenue +47% q/q from $11.56bn; $33.2bn for nine months. Operating loss $567mn vs a $974mn profit in Q2 as opex jumped to $17.5bn from $10.6bn.

  • Main driver: ~$5bn of compute bought on short-term commitments, which cost more than multiyear deals. Price cuts and discounts took ~$600mn off revenue.
  • Net loss $23.3bn, mostly valuation-related accounting charges. Total compute commitments ~$895bn, including Amazon and Google.
  • IPO expected this month. Blackstone expects a "high" junk rating, not investment grade (FT).
  • Bullish: spot GPU sellers and Amazon/Google cloud backlogs. Bearish: IPO pricing into a weak tape with high yields (inference). (The Information, FT)

OpenAI cut its fundraising target to ~$20bn from $30bn. The FT puts the round at a $1.25tn valuation; The Information says the possible valuation was also lowered.

  • Driven by investor anxiety over tech valuations after last week's rout.
  • Read-through: private AI capital is getting choosier, a headwind for neocloud IPOs (inference). (FT, The Information)

AI is now being funded in credit markets, and those markets are wobbling. Junk bonds sold off hard, worsened by liability-management tactics that move assets away from creditors.

  • S&P lowered Meta's credit outlook over AI spending; Amazon disclosed an up-to-$80bn supplier financing loan; Oracle fell 5%+ today on a $10bn stock sale plus $10bn of bonds.
  • Investors are rotating back into Treasuries on fears that US growth leans too hard on AI capex. The 10-year is ~4.88% today, down >4bp.
  • Read-through: wider spreads raise costs for junk-rated AI borrowers, Anthropic included if Blackstone is right (inference). (FT, The Information)

Oil fell 4–7% on hopes of Iran diplomacy, but the Gulf is bracing for the opposite. WTI ~$97–100 after Rubio said the US wants a diplomatic resolution; Brent ~$105–110.

  • Netanyahu is pressing Trump for new strikes; Qatar is mediating an interim US-Iran deal. Gulf diplomats warn renewed fighting would be worse than earlier rounds.
  • US petrol prices are heading for a record on the eve of the midterms.
  • Two-way risk: today's drop prices in de-escalation (inference). (FT)

Binance users pulled ~$5bn as withdrawals reopened after the $6.3bn hack, which the FBI ties to North Korea. Binance says it will repay affected users. (FT, The Information)

The Through-Line

The AI build-out is moving from cash flow to borrowed money: Nvidia guaranteeing neocloud debt, Oracle and Amazon tapping capital markets, Anthropic going public with a junk rating. That makes credit spreads and Treasury yields the new swing factor for AI stocks, just as the junk market wobbles (inference). The industrial logic is unchanged, and Marvell is paying $115bn to own the optics that connect the chips.

Top Takeaways

  1. Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum in an all-stock deal worth ~$115bn (4.5 MRVL shares per LITE share, ~24% premium), because AI data centers are shifting to optical interconnect and Marvell wants to own that layer alongside its networking chips. It is paying in its own stock, so Marvell holders absorb the dilution and a regulatory wait to 1H 2028, and the stock has been marked down for it. (MRVL −13.24% on Oct 29 reports; ~−3% premarket today. LITE: today's move not found)
  2. Nvidia is in talks to guarantee more than $5bn of Nscale debt for a ~460MW campus serving a ~$45bn Anthropic contract, because neoclouds can't borrow cheaply on their own and Nvidia needs them to keep buying GPUs. The mechanism is vendor financing: Nvidia lends its credit rating to its customers, which supports sales now but puts default risk on its own balance sheet. (NVDA: today's move not found)
  3. Anthropic's Q3 revenue rose 47% q/q to ~$17bn, yet it posted a $567mn operating loss vs a $974mn profit in Q2, because it bought ~$5bn of expensive short-term compute and cut prices by ~$600mn to keep growing. Demand is outrunning its long-term capacity, so margins fall just as it prices an IPO this month, and Blackstone expects a high-junk rating. (no public ticker)
  4. OpenAI cut its fundraising target to ~$20bn from $30bn at a $1.25tn valuation, because investors are pulling back on tech after last week's ~$900bn Big Tech rout. Less private money for the top labs means less certainty for the neoclouds and chip orders that depend on their spending (inference). (no public ticker)
  5. AI capex is moving onto balance sheets and into bond markets: Oracle is selling $10bn of stock and $10bn of bonds, Amazon disclosed an up-to-$80bn supplier financing loan, and S&P lowered Meta's credit outlook. When spending runs ahead of operating cash, the cost of capital becomes the constraint, and the junk market is selling off at the same time. (ORCL −5%+ today; AMZN −1.4% Fri; META: today's move not found)
  6. The Nasdaq fell 2.86% on Friday, its worst day since June, as Intel dropped 28.6%, its worst since 1974, on a weak Q4 guide, and Amazon's capex worries spread. Stocks bounced today (S&P +1.0%, Nasdaq +1.3% by late morning), but the market is still repricing AI spenders on cash and credit, not on revenue growth. (INTC −28.6% Fri)
  7. Oil fell 4–7% today (WTI ~$97–100, Brent ~$105–110) after Rubio signalled the US wants a diplomatic end to the Iran conflict, while Netanyahu presses for new strikes. Cheaper crude eases inflation and rate pressure going into Tuesday's midterms, but the drop assumes de-escalation that Gulf diplomats doubt. (energy is the weakest S&P sector today)
  8. Investors are rotating into Treasuries (10-year ~4.88%, down >4bp) on fears that US growth relies too much on AI spending, even as October ISM manufacturing beat at 55.5 and prices paid eased to 75.2. A strong economy with softer price pressure is good news for bonds only if the Fed, priced ~85% for a December hike as of Oct 29, sees it the same way. (no single ticker)
  9. Binance users pulled ~$5bn as withdrawals resumed after the $6.3bn hack the FBI ties to North Korea. Exchange trust, not price, is the risk: outflows move to regulated venues and self-custody (inference). (no public ticker)
